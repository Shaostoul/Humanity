Widget Playground

A sandbox for the interface's building blocks - the ones the real app uses, and experimental ones we're trying out. Everything here is inert - nothing you click, hold, or toggle triggers a real action, so it's a safe place to test that the effects work, prototype new widgets, and learn how each one behaves and why.

Press-and-hold confirm

Actions you can't take back - erasing your server mailbox, disbanding a group, deleting all your data - are guarded by a press-and-HOLD instead of a single click. A stray tap does nothing; you have to hold until the meter fills. Releasing early cancels. This is the one thing that turns a fat-fingered misclick from a disaster into a non-event.

Channeling (RGB) border

The cycling rainbow border marks something that is live or active - the current nav tab, or the Download button when a new version is ready. It's decorative signal, not noise: it only appears on the one thing that's "on". It also respects accessibility: turning on Reduce Motion / No RGB in Settings holds it to a steady colour.

Attention badge

A gently pulsing badge draws the eye to something new without stealing focus - an unread count, or an available update. The pulse is slow on purpose; a fast flash would be stressful and hurt accessibility.

Cistercian numerals (experimental)

A medieval monk's shorthand: one glyph encodes 0 to 9999 on a single vertical stave, one digit per corner (units top-right, tens top-left, hundreds bottom-right, thousands bottom-left; 0 draws nothing). We push it further here: past 9999 it chains glyphs in base-10000, so any number fits. The idea is learning by comparison, the same number shown at once as the glyph(s), as the decimal you already read, and in binary, with a key to all nine symbols. Drag the slider or type a number and watch them rebuild together.
