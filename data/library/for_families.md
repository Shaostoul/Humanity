# HumanityOS for Families

## The squeeze you already know

Groceries cost more every year. Screens are everywhere, and most of them
are built to farm your kids' attention, not to give anything back. And the
practical skills that used to pass from grandparent to parent to child,
growing food, fixing what breaks, making a budget stretch, are quietly
disappearing from family life.

None of that is your family's failure. It is the environment every
household is raising kids in now. HumanityOS is a free set of tools built
to push back on all three at once.

## Screen time that gives something back

HumanityOS includes a game world that teaches real homesteading: growing
food, managing water and power, building things, keeping a household
running on a budget of real resources. The plants, recipes, and materials in it
are modeled on the real ones, so what a child learns tending a virtual
garden walks straight off the screen and into your actual garden and
kitchen.

Honesty first: the game is in active development and is not fully playable
yet. We would rather tell you that plainly than have you download it
expecting a finished world. What families can use together today are the
real-life tools: shared task lists, maps, chat with end-to-end encrypted
private messages, the project's library, and a marketplace for trading
person to person (young and quiet yet). The game is growing alongside
them, in the open, where anyone can watch it happen.

## Your household as a team

Most family software treats each person as a separate account to be
managed. HumanityOS treats the household as what it actually is: a small
team working on the same life.

Shared tasks let everyone see what needs doing and who is on it. Garden
planning becomes something you do together at the table, not a chore one
adult carries alone. A child who checks off "water the tomatoes" is not
doing homework. They are holding up a real corner of the family.

This is the homestead idea scaled to one household: a family that can meet
more of its own needs is harder to squeeze, and every skill learned at
home is one less thing you have to pay for or ask permission for. The
longer version of that argument is here:
https://united-humanity.us/library#why-homesteading-works

## Safety and dignity, by design

You should not have to trade your children's privacy for useful software.
Here is where HumanityOS stands:

- No ads. Nothing in it is trying to sell your kids anything.
- No data harvesting. There are no accounts, no tracking, and nothing
  collected to sell. Private messages are encrypted so that even the
  people running the server cannot read them.
- Chat only connects when you open it. Nothing auto-connects your family
  to anyone. Be aware the community server is public, like a town square:
  members can post and message each other. A family that wants a fully
  private space can run its own server, because the whole system is
  self-hostable, and then no stranger can reach it at all.
- Everything is inspectable. The entire project is open source and public
  domain. Any parent, or any programmer a parent trusts, can read exactly
  what the software does. You do not have to take our word for any of
  this: https://github.com/Shaostoul/Humanity

We hold ourselves to this because the mission demands it, not because a
policy page says so. The full mission is here:
https://united-humanity.us/mission

## What it costs, honestly

Nothing. HumanityOS is free and released into the public domain, which
means it belongs to everyone, forever, and can never be sold back to you.

There is no company behind it and no nonprofit. It is built by a
maintainer and volunteers, and it runs on personal gifts. If your family
ever chooses to support it, that support is a gift to a person, not a
purchase and not a tax-deductible donation. We will not dress that up as
something it is not: https://united-humanity.us/donate

## Passing something down

Teach a child to grow one plant and something shifts. They watered it,
they watched it, it lived because of what they did. A child who has done
that once knows, in a way no lecture can teach, that they can affect the
world with their own hands.

That belief compounds. One plant becomes a garden bed. A garden bed
becomes a kid who checks the soil before anyone asks. Years later it
becomes an adult who looks at a broken thing and thinks "I can probably
fix that" instead of "I need to pay someone."

That is the inheritance we are trying to help families pass down. Not
software. Capability.
