## The aurora was lying down because it was 85 km too low

His hypothesis was a rotation axis. It was the altitude, and the two are
indistinguishable from any distance, which is why it survived a release.

Band 2 stores its emitting layer as a fraction of the atmosphere shell
thickness, and the aurora shipped with 0.08 to 0.78. Nobody had converted that
to kilometres. Doing it: Earth ships `atmosphere_scale` 0.015, so `rp = 1/1.03`
in shell units, one shell unit is `6371 / 0.9709 = 6562 km`, and the whole shell
is `(1 - 0.9709) * 6562 = 191 km` thick. The layer therefore ran from **15 km to
149 km**, starting in the stratosphere and topping out below a low orbit.

He flew to 155 km. He was above the entire aurora looking down at a sheet, and a
sheet seen from above is exactly what "laying on its side" describes.

Now 0.52 to 0.995, which is 99 km to 190 km. 99 km is the real sharp lower
border of the green.

**The general lesson is not about auroras.** A payload expressed as a fraction
of something is unreadable on its own. Both numbers looked like sensible
fractions and the render looked like an aurora. The altitude note now lives in
`data/environment/region_kinds.ron` beside the numbers, and the memory file
`feedback_convert_params_to_real_units` carries the rule.

**No fixture could have caught it.** Every aurora fixture sat at 1500 km or
higher, where a layer on the ground and a layer at 100 km are a pixel apart. A
fixture set that cannot distinguish two very different values of a parameter
does not cover that parameter, however many fixtures it has. Added
`aurora-oval-300`, `aurora-limb-600`, `aurora-limb-1200` and its `-t45` twin.

## Shape and motion

The oval was one smooth band. It is now three strands, each meandering in
latitude along its own path of three incommensurate harmonics (3, 7 and 13 lobes,
so the sum never repeats over a circuit), each present only along part of the
oval. Where two cross they merge; where a presence mask closes, the web has a
hole. That is his "spider webbing", and it is what the reference photographs
show.

Rays went from 47 lobes to 1200. 47 around a 40,000 km circle is one "ray" every
850 km, which is a lobe of the oval, not a ray. 1200 puts them at about 34 km.
They fade with distance to their own exact mean (5/16, the mean of
`pow(0.5 + 0.5 sin, 3)`), so losing the detail at range costs no brightness.
That fade reads the camera, and the comment says why that is legitimate here:
it is antialiasing, which has nothing else it could read, not a weight on the
effect, which may never read the camera (BUG-080).

On motion, both halves of his question were right and the old version only had
the slow half. The oval genuinely does drift over hours: a feature crossing it
at about 1 km/s takes hours to travel round, so `AURORA_DRIFT` stays small. The
fine structure does not: rays shimmer and pulsating patches brighten and fade
over 1 to 20 seconds, and that is the motion the eye reads as alive. Those run
an order of magnitude faster. Measured directly from the curtain math: 29% of
its own brightness in one second, 40% in two.

Colour was rebalanced. Red had seventy percent of the curtain and dominated it;
every photograph is green-dominated because 557.7 nm is the brightest auroral
line by a wide margin. Green now fills the lower two thirds and stops, red caps
the top third at 0.30 rather than 0.55.

**Known limit, deliberate.** The real red cap reaches 300 to 400 km and this
cannot, because the emission is clipped to the atmosphere chord and the shell
mesh ends at 191 km. Enlarging the shell is cheap in physics (`shell_packing`
derives both `rp` and `h_rel` from the same scale, and at 191 km with an 8.5 km
scale height the added chord is `exp(-22)` of vacuum) and expensive in blast
radius. It is its own increment, noted in `docs/design/environment-fields.md`.

## The build not reaching him, raised for the second time

The machinery was already right: `archive-build.js` refreshes a stable
`HumanityOS.exe` at the repo root for the taskbar. The failure is that shipping
can skip it, and **every check we had measured a different question**.
`just brief` compared Cargo.toml to the newest GitHub release, CI compared the
push to the VPS, the tag compared to itself. All three truthfully read "in sync
at v0.1331" while the exe on his taskbar was v0.1326.

That is the part worth keeping: a green check on an adjacent question is worse
than no check, because it ends the investigation.

So the exe now carries a build stamp (version, time, git head), written only
after the copy succeeds so it can never claim a delivery that did not happen.
`scripts/check-delivery.js` reads it and separates NOT BUILT from NOT DELIVERED,
because those need different fixes. `just brief` prints a DELIVERY row.
`just deliver` is build plus archive without the version bump.

Two subtleties encoded deliberately: a version bump is NOT a code change (every
ship bumps, so counting it would cry wolf daily and the warning would stop
meaning anything within a day), and a shader edit is NOT a rebuild, because
`shader_loader` prefers an `assets/` directory found beside the exe and the
taskbar copy sits at the repo root next to `assets/`.

The gate was proven able to fail before being trusted: it read NO STAMP and NOT
BUILT before, and Delivery OK after. It then immediately caught a real break, a
backslash a bash heredoc had eaten out of `archive-build.js`, which had made
`just build-game` fail at its archive step.

`archive-build.js` also now rejects unknown flags. The no-flag branch archives,
and archiving kills a running HumanityOS to take the file lock, so a typo like
`--help` used to shut the game down and silently re-archive. That happened while
investigating.

## The white specks are the known TV static

Not a new defect. A zoom into `approach-2000km-high` shows it exactly as
PRIORITIES describes: cloud masses with correct coherent silhouettes whose
interiors are pure black-and-white salt and pepper.

Three things got settled.

**A fifth hypothesis refuted.** `orbit-2000-high-notemporal` had been built in a
previous pass and never captured, and PRIORITIES named it as the experiment that
would say whether the accumulator refuses to converge the sun term. Captured,
with a new `scripts/speckle-census.js`:

| arm | speckle |
| --- | --- |
| `approach-2000km-high` baseline | 1.76% |
| accumulation off | 1.88% |
| Low tier, same camera | **0.63%** |

The accumulator is not the carrier. The per-pixel jitter is.

**Why it "does not rotate with the planet", which is the useful clue.** All
three jitters were frozen in v0.1253.2 after the operator's own on/off
experiment, because a frame-advancing jitter made a parked frame fizz. Frozen
means keyed on `in.pos.xy`, the SCREEN pixel. So the grain is nailed to the
screen while the world slides underneath it. Parked it looks like fine texture;
in motion it reads exactly as he described. The freeze did not reduce the noise,
it changed which reference frame the noise lives in.

**A stale comment that would have misled the next reader.**
`cloud_layer_volumetric` still claimed the temporal composite is "where the
boiling static dies: the map is an exponential average of many jittered
marches". That map was RETIRED in v0.1250; `frame_shells.rs` pins
`near_mix = 1.0` and the octa pass never dispatches. Confirmed live:
`[CloudRegime]` reports `temporal=true mix=1.00` at every altitude captured,
0.8 km to 35,870 km. Nothing averages the march any more, which is why four
accumulator-shaped hypotheses have all failed. Comment corrected.

**And a sixth hypothesis refuted, with the control that made it trustworthy.**
`cloud_sun_tau` spirals its cone taps by a per-pixel azimuth, which looks like
the carrier and is inside the one term the bisect blames. Removing it:

| arm | mean L | speckle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| baseline | 128.8 | 1.80% |
| per-pixel cone azimuth removed | 128.8 | 1.76% |
| POSITIVE CONTROL, cone spread 0.12 to 2.50 | 119.9 | **1.39%** |

The azimuth does nothing. The control is the row that matters: same constant
block, same code path, and it moved the frame hard, so the null is a real null
rather than an edit that never reached the GPU.

The control also points at the fix. Widening the cone LOWERED the grain, so the
carrier is how `tau_sun` is sampled, not how the taps are arranged. And the
march advances its first step by the jittered fraction
(`t_cur = t_cur + dt * max(jitter, 1e-3)`), which phase-shifts the whole sample
comb per pixel, so a per-pixel depth offset becomes a per-pixel `tau_sun`.
Next experiment, fenced in PRIORITIES: snap the sun ladder origin to the
unjittered step grid while leaving the eye ray jittered.

**And then that experiment was run rather than left as a plan, because the
mechanism turned out to be one line.** Starting the sun ladder from the
unjittered step centre moved speckle 1.80 to 1.54 percent and raised mean
brightness 128.8 to 138.8. The direction is confirmed; the magnitude is a
seventh of the way to Low, and the 8 percent brightness rise is a look
regression, because the grid origin traverses a systematically less occluded
column so clouds self-shadow less. Reverted rather than shipped.

Two readings remain, both in PRIORITIES. Give `cloud_sun_tau` two origins (the
true sample for the near rungs where the rind must self-shadow exactly, the grid
for the far rungs where the variance is). Or measure the resolve filter in
isolation: the noise is white PCG, the v0.1252 note says white noise is exactly
what a local mean annihilates, and if that filter is gated or fooled by
cloud-edge contrast it explains every failed hypothesis at once. The second is
cheaper and explains more, so it goes first.

Nothing of the experiment shipped; the shader was restored to the committed
state and verified clean.

## v0.1331.6: four copies of one bind group

`just verify` had been RED since the environment-region work landed, and nobody
noticed because the failure predates any commit that would have been blamed:
`frame_shells.rs` 1330 lines over a 1300 budget, `renderer/mod.rs` 2875 over
2800.

The lint says, in its own failure message, not to raise the budget but to move
the code where it belongs. The biggest piece turned out to be worth more than
its line count. `camera_bind_group_layout` declares five bindings, and mod.rs
listed all five by hand in FOUR places: the main camera group, the shadow pass's
light camera, the lights regrow, and the env-regions regrow. Adding binding 4
meant editing all four.

That is the v0.1029 incident's exact shape one layout earlier: a texture binding
added, two of three sites updated, and the missed one built lazily so menu-only
boots stayed green for ten releases while every world entry died. There is now
one `pipeline::camera_bind_group`, and a class of bug stops being possible
rather than merely documented.

`env_regions::storage_buffer` replaces two hand-rolled allocations, and is a
function precisely because the buffer must be zero filled: `create_buffer`
leaves contents undefined, and kind 0 is the reserved empty row every consumer
skips, so a zeroed buffer is inert and undefined memory is not.
`push_auroral_ovals` and `log_regions` moved out of the frame loop.

Result: 2875 to 2791 and 1330 to 1298, both under budget, no behaviour changed.
Verified with `cargo check` on both feature sets, `just verify` green, and the
probe rig entering the world, because a bind-group change is the one kind a
static check cannot clear.

## The clouds were darker than the desert (v0.1331.8)

The session ended somewhere it did not start. After SEVEN refuted hypotheses
about the orbital static, all of them about noise, one measurement reframed it:
cloud luminance against TERRAIN IN THE SAME FRAME.

| tier | cloud | terrain | ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| High, the default | 120.1 | 174.9 | **0.69** |
| Low | 189.2 | 175.9 | 1.08 |

Noon over the Sahara. Terrain is the control and is unchanged, so the tiers
differ in the clouds alone. Cloud albedo is 0.7 to 0.9 against sand at about
0.35, so a daylit deck must read BRIGHTER than the desert. The default tier
rendered it 31 percent darker.

The cause was that MULTIPLE SCATTERING WAS OFF. What makes a real cloud bright
is light bouncing inside it many times; a single-scattering Beer-Lambert march
is dark by construction. The term existed, built v0.1288, and was parked by its
own commit with the note that it stays off until the interior gate is met by
physics, because at a close camera it reached 58 against a target of 135 or
more. It was judged on the INTERIOR and nobody asked what it does from orbit.
Parking it on one criterion left a worse defect on the tier every user gets.

On, at unit gain: ratio 0.69 to 1.07 against Low tier own 1.08; terrain 174.9
to 175.3; speckle 1.76 to 1.00 percent WITH NO FILTER CHANGE; interior 49.9 to
108.3; cost gpu.cloud_screen 5.78 to 5.76 ms on armed timestamps.

Two things about that table are worth keeping. Unit gain matches an INDEPENDENT
path rather than taste (gain 2.0 overshoots at 1.16). And the speckle row is
what demotes the whole noise arc: adding light cut the grain 43 percent while
touching nothing that filters, which is the definition of a symptom.

It also makes the operator original report legible. He wrote, in v0.1252,
"awfully dark and white in spots to the point of looking like old TV static".
That is TWO symptoms. Every session since, including the first half of this
one, read only the second.

Still unfixed, and stated rather than buried: the interior gate remains unmet
(108.3 against 135+) because the modelled deck is optically thinner than real
cumulus, which is density work; and residual orbital speckle is 1.00 against
Low 0.63, kept as PRIORITIES item 2b with the filter measurements intact.

New metric, and the reason this was findable at all:
scripts/cloud-brightness.js. It reports cloud luminance against terrain
luminance in the same frame. The terrain is the control, sharing sun, exposure,
tonemap and atmosphere, which is what made the tier comparison conclusive
instead of suggestive.

## The unattended stretch (v0.1331.11 to .16)

The operator went away and asked for work that did not need him. Six releases,
all documentation and tooling: every code experiment in this stretch was
reverted, and the only shipped code change is one to a check.

### The aurora root cause

The deck sits at 12 km and the aurora emits between 99 and 190 km, so from
above the clouds are BEHIND it. The fullscreen cloud composite runs after the
transparent list that carries the atmosphere shell, so it paints over the
emission anyway. Measured at lat 67, nadir, 600 km, local midnight, differing
only in longitude, as mean green-excess:

| arm | clouds ON | clouds OFF |
| --- | --- | --- |
| over land | 4.288 | **14.811** |
| over water | 2.198 | **14.826** |

Clouds off makes the arms IDENTICAL. So the land/water difference he reported
is cloud cover wearing a coastline, and the aurora is dimmed 3.4x over land and
6.7x over water. He saw something real and named it by the nearest landmark.

Fenced as PRIORITIES 1b rather than fixed: it needs the camera uniform and the
env_regions buffer reaching a pass that has neither, and `atmo_over` is always
false for a documented reason whose regression cost a dozen investigations.

### Two of my own claims corrected

The v0.1331.10 commit said the land/water frames were "DAYLIT". They were at
local midnight the whole time; a camera block with lat/lon converts `time` to
local solar hour already. They were bright because they AIMED at the horizon.

And v0.1331.14 said the spatial filter strength cap was the binding constraint.
At the terminator it is not: raising it alone moves 40.38 to 40.22.

### The grain lives at the terminator

Profiled across the terminator: 0.55 percent in full daylight, 40.43 at the
dusk line, about 4 on the night side. The operator had already said exactly
that with no instrument.

That invalidated the vantage EVERY earlier speckle number used, including all
of that morning: they were taken at noon, band 0, the weakest regime by a
factor of seventy. And the ranking inverts between regimes:

| candidate | at noon | at the dusk line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| sun ladder on the grid | 14% better | **0.4%** |
| animated jitter | 9% better | **78%** |

### And the metric itself is gameable

Forcing the spatial filter to full scored best of all (6.07 percent), but grain
here is deviation from the four-neighbour mean and that arm replaces every
pixel WITH the neighbour mean. A box blur minimises the metric by construction.
The animated-jitter number is honest because temporal averaging does not blur,
which makes 8.68 the best trustworthy result and leaves the fizz trade (2.09
percent against 0.34) as the operator decision, stated with both numbers and
not taken unilaterally.

### A near-miss worth keeping

An archive went out carrying an experimental gate that had already been
reverted in source, because `check-delivery.js` treated all of
`assets/shaders/` as live-loaded. Only `assets/shaders/pbr/` is; the rest are
`include_str!` with no disk path. Caught by hand, the archive rebuilt from
reverted source and re-verified at 40.44 against the 40.38 baseline, and the
check fixed and proven in both directions.

Same shape as the bug that check was written for two days earlier: the rule was
right for the case its author had in mind and silently wrong for one that was
never checked.

### What the day is really about

Six of the operator’s observations turned out to be precisely correct, and in
four of them he named the mechanism’s signature without any instrument: the
aurora "laying on its side" (it was 85 km too low), "glistening ... most
noticeable at the dusk line" (73x worse there), the land/water split (cloud
cover), and "awfully dark and white in spots" (two symptoms, and every session
since had read only one of them).

The recurring failure on this side was never analysis. It was choosing where to
measure. Picking the vantage IS the experiment.
