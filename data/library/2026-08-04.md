## The five mechanisms

**The tufts were geometrically cubes.** Every foliage card in a sleeve took the
twig axis as its up-vector, so every card PLANE contained the twig direction and
every card NORMAL lay in the single plane perpendicular to it. Measured per
sleeve: fir and pine were **100% parallel pairs** (two coplanar slabs), the
broadleaves **67% perpendicular + 33% parallel**, every other angle bin empty.
Uniform-on-a-sphere puts 18.9% in those two bins.

Two changes, both load-bearing. Golden-angle azimuths (137.507 deg, real
alternate phyllotaxis) spread the normals AROUND a circle. That alone still left
33% of every 4-card tuft within 5 degrees of perpendicular, because they were all
still ON that circle. The second half tilts each card off it — and stratifying
those tilts by equal solid angle, the correct answer for scattering POINTS,
measured WORSE than no tilt at all (42%), because a sleeve is a set of PLANES and
a plane is the same plane whichever way its normal points. Solving it as a line
packing (Tammes in real projective space) got every species to 0.000.

**Distant cards were 4-8x too bright**, from three multiplying causes: a card lit
as a flat plate returns 1.0 with the sun overhead where a real crown averages
0.18-0.36; the bake decoded the crown-depth shading out of the uv and then threw
it away; and an up-facing normal takes the maximum sky weight. Separately, the
far forest sheet was drawing the planet's GROUND imagery with full ground PBR, so
distant woods read as grass and dirt.

**The float-up/fall-down bug was frame rate, not pitch.** The frame-lock
un-rotated the camera with THIS frame's spin, but the camera was placed by LAST
frame's co-rotation, so terrain was sampled one frame of planetary rotation
behind the player's feet. On the 20-minute day that is 33,360 m/s of ground slip:
1.1 km at 30 fps, 4.7 km at 7. The altitude reading grew with frame time, crossed
the drawn-ground gate, and flipped stand height by 2.45 m. Looking at vegetation
costs frames — so pitch was a proxy for load, never the input. v0.1108 had
patched the same defect at the symptom.

**The card-hide radius promised models that never drew** — see BUG-068. Three
successive rules each computed a PROXY for a promise instead of the promise.

**Forest density was a process-global read separately by two streams** — see
BUG-069.

## Two things I told the operator wrongly

Recorded because the wrong answers were plausible and someone will re-derive them.

1. **The conifer card over-brightness is not anisotropic fir foliage.** I said it
   was real botany: flat sprays along a branch, narrow crown. It is the ODD
   (first-moment) component of the spherified shading normals — a crown's
   area-weighted mean normal is nonzero (fir 0.1221 down to momiji 0.0217) and
   that ordering matches the brightness ordering exactly. Cancelling it lands
   every species at 1.00, flat across the sky. NOTE an adversarial reviewer
   REFUTED the geometric story offered for *why* the mean points down while
   reproducing the arithmetic, so the first moment is measured, not derived.
2. **The density rounding divergence is not the cause of the "splotchy chunks".**
   Real (up to ~30% of cells disagree by one tree at bad slider values) but it
   flips about one tree in 400 — nowhere near chunk scale.

## Process lessons

**`git apply --3way` STAGES what it applies.** After applying three worktree
patches, `git add <three files>` added to an index that already held all nine, so
the commit swept every lane under a message describing one — the exact failure
the shared-checkout rule exists to prevent. Caught by reading `git show --stat`
AFTER committing, which is too late. Rule: after any `git apply`, `git reset`
before staging, and read `git diff --cached --stat` BEFORE the commit.

**Judge a worktree by its own change, not by whether it differs from main.** The
first worktree audit compared files against main and concluded 19 of 19 might
hold work. They differ because main MOVED. Diffing each worktree against its
merge-base with main showed 9 fully merged and 10 holding only superseded drafts.

**A per-worktree tool must confirm the directory IS a worktree.** `git -C <dir>`
inside a directory with no `.git` of its own walks UP and answers about the
enclosing repo, so an empty husk reported a plausible-looking merge-base.

**A gate can be too blunt to see the defect it exists for.** The first box gate
grouped cards by proximity and reported 0.31 against a 0.30 threshold, a near
false-pass, where the exact per-sleeve grouping showed 1.00 against 0.19.

## Verification pattern that worked

Every fix this session was implemented in an isolated worktree, then reviewed by
an independent agent whose first job was to REPRODUCE the red proof rather than
trust it. Three reviews, three independent reproductions, one blocking finding
(a false claim in a comment about a shipped default). That catch alone justified
the pass.
