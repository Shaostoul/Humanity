# What is not allowed on HumanityOS servers, and how to report it

**Last changed 20 November 2026.** This is the content policy of the HumanityOS project, of its own
server at united-humanity.us, and of public.guide, run by the same maintainer. It says what is
never allowed, how anyone can report it, what a server's operator does about a report, and what
private, sealed messages mean for all of this. It is not legal advice, and nobody who wrote it is a
lawyer. Where it says what the law asks, it leans on dated research from the law's own text, linked
at the end.

Anyone can run a HumanityOS server: the software is free. Each server is run by a person or a group
who answers to the law where they are, and who may add stricter rules of their own. We ask every
operator to follow this policy.

## The short version

- **Child sexual abuse material is never allowed, anywhere on HumanityOS.** Neither is any sexual
  contact with a child, or any attempt at it.
- **Neither is anything else the Humanity Accord forbids absolutely**, nor anything illegal where
  the server runs.
- **If a child or anyone else is in danger right now, call your local emergency number first** (911
  in the United States).
- **To report something on HumanityOS**, use Report on one of the person's messages, in your
  conversation with them, or on their name in the member list. **To report child sexual
  exploitation to the authorities**, anyone in any country can use the CyberTipline at
  [report.cybertip.org](https://report.cybertip.org/) or call 1-800-843-5678.
- **Never save, screenshot or forward an abusive picture or video, not even to report it.** If you
  already have one, say so in your report instead of sending it.
- **Private messages are sealed.** Nobody on a server can read them or scan them, so action on a
  private conversation starts with a report from someone in it.

## What is not allowed

### First: the sexual exploitation of children

A child is anyone under 18. None of this is allowed, in public or in private, in pictures, video,
writing, a drawing, or anything a computer or an AI made:

- any sexual picture or video of a child, real or made-up, drawn, edited or generated;
- talking to a child about sex, or sending a child sexual pictures or messages;
- asking a child for pictures of themselves, or to keep a secret with you;
- arranging to meet a child for sex, or offering, selling or trading a child for sex.

On united-humanity.us and public.guide, this means removal at once, a ban, and a report to the
authorities, with no warning first and no appeal on that server. In the United States the law
treats these pictures as contraband: having them, or looking them up on purpose to see them, is a
crime, and that includes computer-made pictures that look like a real child.

### Then: everything else the Humanity Accord forbids absolutely

The Humanity Accord names a few acts as wrong without exception, whatever anyone's intent, culture
or claimed authority ([Absolute Prohibitions](/library#absolute-prohibitions)). On HumanityOS
that means no using the platform to do, arrange, threaten or profit from any of them, including:

- sexual pictures or videos of a person shared without their consent, or a recording of a sexual
  assault;
- buying, selling or trafficking people, or forced labour;
- threats to kill or hurt someone, and material made by torturing or abusing someone;
- blackmail, including threatening to share someone's pictures unless they pay or send more;
- fraud and theft: scams, stealing someone's account, tricking people out of money or their
  recovery phrase;
- treating people as less than human: calls to harm a group of people for who they are.

### And: anything illegal where the server runs

Each server also follows the law of the place it runs, and its operator may set stricter rules. The
rules for united-humanity.us are at [united-humanity.us/rules](https://united-humanity.us/rules).

## How to report something

### If someone is in danger right now

Call your local emergency number before anything else: 911 in the United States. A server's admins
are volunteers, not police, and cannot send help.

### On HumanityOS

Use **Report** on one of the person's messages, in your conversation with them, or on their name in
the member list, in the desktop app or on the website. Choose the reason that fits. Choosing **"A
child may be in danger"** or **"Someone is in danger"** also shows, right there in the report
window, the emergency number and the official reporting lines for the country you pick. The app
never looks up where you are; the country stays on your device.

What happens to your report:

- It goes to the admins and moderators of that server. The person you report is not told who
  reported them; moderators see only "a member", and the admins see who you are.
- From a private conversation, you can tick that person's messages to send with the report. Each
  carries the sender's signature, so the admins can check the messages really came from them. They
  cannot check anything else: the time on a message is the sender's own clock, and you chose which
  messages to include.
- **A message that carries a picture or a file cannot be sent with a report**, on purpose: nobody on
  a server should ever be shown such a picture. Report it to the authorities instead (below).
- A report about a group's message can go to the group's creator, the server's admins, or both.
- "Also block them" is ticked for you when you report from a private conversation.

### Straight to the authorities

You do not need to report on HumanityOS first, and you do not need to be a member of anything.

- **The CyberTipline** ([report.cybertip.org](https://report.cybertip.org/), or 1-800-843-5678, day
  and night), run by the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children, takes reports of child
  sexual exploitation from anyone, in any country. You do not have to give your name. Every report
  is made available to law enforcement.
- **Outside the United States**, your country may have its own hotline. The report window in the
  app lists them for many countries, and the [INHOPE member list](https://www.inhope.org/EN/our-members)
  finds the one for yours.
- **The police**, where you are.

### What not to do

- Do not save, download, screenshot or forward an abusive picture or video, and do not show it to
  anyone, even to prove what happened. The CyberTipline's own advice is not to attach screenshots
  but to say in your report that you have them; it may then contact you.

## What a server's operator does about a report

This is the process on united-humanity.us and public.guide, and the one we ask of every operator. In the
United States, a provider of a communication service that learns of apparent child sexual abuse
material must report it to the CyberTipline and keep what it reported for one year (federal law, 18
U.S.C. 2258A). Whether a volunteer running a small server counts as such a provider is a question
for a lawyer; we act as if it does, and we ask every operator to do the same.

When a report says a child may be involved, the operator:

1. **Calls the emergency number first** if a child may be in danger right now.
2. **Never opens, downloads, copies, screenshots or forwards** a reported picture or file, and does
   not show it to other admins. (The app never shows admins a file or a link from a report, for this
   reason.)
3. **Takes it out of public view at once**, without destroying it, and bans the person's key. In
   both apps, **Hide and keep** on the report does this and step 5 together (below).
4. **Reports it to the CyberTipline** (or, outside the United States, to their country's official
   line). An operator can register with the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children as a
   provider (espteam@ncmec.org) and report as one; anyone can use the public form.
5. **Keeps what was reported, locked away, for one year**, where only the operator can reach it,
   then deletes it. Two things change the year. If the police or another part of the government
   asks for it to be preserved, it is kept while that request lasts (90 days, and 90 more if they
   ask again). If law enforcement asks for it to be destroyed, it is destroyed. The law asks a
   provider to keep it securely and to let as few people reach it as possible.
6. **Tells nobody else what it contains.** What was reported may go only to the National Center for
   Missing & Exploited Children, to the police, or in answer to a court order or similar legal
   process.

For every other report, the admins decide in proportion: dismiss it, warn, mute, kick or ban the
person, or delete the reported post. The rules page of united-humanity.us says who moderates it
and what each of those does.

**Hide and keep** (both apps, on a report about a post, for admins and moderators) takes the post
and every picture or file it linked out of every view at once, and keeps one copy sealed so that
only the server's owner can open it (and the one successor the owner may name, who can open it only
once they hold the owner role themselves). Nobody can view the copy in the app, the owner included. Under
Server Settings, **Held copies** shows the admins how many copies are kept and when each one is
deleted (a year after it was held), never what they hold. Only the owner can place a **legal hold**
(when a preservation request arrives), **destroy** a copy now (when law enforcement asks),
**export** it (the owner's own device opens it straight into one file, for the CyberTipline or the
police, never on screen) or **restore** it if a report proves false. Every one of those steps is
signed by the person who took it and logged for every admin to see.

### What the app does not do for an operator yet

We would rather say it than have an operator find out at the wrong moment:

- **A server needs an owner before it can keep a copy.** Hide and keep seals its copy to the
  owner's key card, so it is refused, with nothing kept, until the server has an owner whose key
  card it holds. Whoever runs the server claims it: the server prints a one-time claim code to its
  own log (and to `data/owner-claim-code.txt` on the server machine), and they paste it into Server
  Settings, Claim ownership, after signing in once so their key card arrives. The owner can later
  hand the role to another member, and name one successor; both re-seal every kept copy first.
- **Reports are deleted 90 days after a decision**, unless they hold a kept copy. A report left
  open is kept.
- So, on a server whose owner has not claimed it yet: first move any uploaded file, without opening it, out of
  the server's uploads folder (`data/uploads/`) into a folder only the operator can reach. Then use
  "Delete the post" on the report, not the ordinary Delete on the message, which also deletes the
  files only that post linked. If you reported it to the CyberTipline, copy the post's words from
  the report beside the file and keep both for the year. When in doubt, ask a lawyer or the
  CyberTipline's provider team.
- **The app does not tell the person whose post was hidden** that it was kept, nor that anything was
  reported; a ban says only what a ban says. This is decided (2026-11-20): the person is told nothing
  beyond the ban message (`docs/design/hide-and-keep.md`).
- **Restore puts a post back on this server only.** Linked servers that dropped their copy do not
  get it back.

## What sealed private messages mean for this

Private messages and private group chats on HumanityOS are end-to-end encrypted: sealed on the
sender's device and opened only on the recipient's. The server keeps a sealed private message so it
can be delivered, addressed only to its recipient, with no sender written down, and deletes it
after 30 days by default ([Who Can Read Your Messages](/library#who-can-read-your-messages)).

- **Nobody on a server can read them**: not the admins, not the operator, not us.
- **Nothing scans them**, on the server or on your device, for anyone else. This is our choice, by
  design, so that no server can read people's private lives. It is not forced on us by a law or a
  platform's rule; and US federal law does not require a provider to monitor messages or scan for
  this material (18 U.S.C. 2258A(f)). The warnings the app can show on messages run on your own
  device and tell no one.
- **So action starts from a report by someone in the conversation.** You hold your own messages, so
  you can report them: your report can carry the other person's signed messages, and the admins can
  check who wrote them. An admin can then ban that person from the server, and words alone (an
  adult talking to a child about sex, for example) can be enough for the operator to report to the
  CyberTipline.
- **Pictures in a private conversation stay between its members.** A server cannot see them, and a
  report cannot carry them. Report them straight to the CyberTipline and say that you have them; do
  not forward them.

## If you think a decision was wrong

Each server's operator decides how decisions there can be questioned. On united-humanity.us, the
sexual exploitation of children has no appeal on that server, and every other decision can be
questioned; its rules page says how ([united-humanity.us/rules](https://united-humanity.us/rules)).
