# Draft: asking NCMEC whether a volunteer-run server should register to report

**Drafted 20 November 2026. Nothing has been sent.** This is a draft for the project's maintainer
to send himself, from his own address, if he chooses. It asks the question the dated finding could
not answer from public sources: whether a volunteer running a small HumanityOS server is a
"provider" that should register with the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC)
to report to its CyberTipline
([`docs/reference/findings/2026-11-20-csam-reporting.md`](../reference/findings/2026-11-20-csam-reporting.md),
"What is still unknown"). NCMEC's page on child sexual abuse material sends providers who want to
register to espteam@ncmec.org.

An answer from NCMEC is helpful but is not legal advice; a lawyer's opinion is the only thing that
settles the question. When an answer comes, record it (with its date) in a new finding.

---

**To:** espteam@ncmec.org

**Subject:** Should a volunteer-run chat server register to report to the CyberTipline?

Hello,

I maintain HumanityOS, free and open-source software for chat, community and everyday tools. Anyone
can run a HumanityOS server. I run two myself, united-humanity.us and public.guide, as one private
person: there is no company, no staff and no income from them. People post in public and
members-only rooms and can upload pictures and files there. Private messages and private group
chats are end-to-end encrypted, so the server cannot read them, and nothing scans them. Members can
report a person to the server's admins from a message, a conversation or the member list.

We have written a content policy that treats child sexual abuse material as never allowed and sends
people to the CyberTipline. I would like to get the reporting side right before the platform has
many users, so I have a few questions:

1. Do you register individuals or small volunteer-run services as electronic service providers, so
   they can report as providers? Would you recommend that a server like mine registers?
2. If so, what does registering involve, and what contact details do you need from us?
3. Until we are registered, is the public CyberTipline form the right way for us to report
   something we learn of on our servers?
4. Do you have guidance for very small providers on keeping reported material for the year after a
   report, securely and with access limited, when there is no staff?
5. Other people run their own HumanityOS servers, in the United States and elsewhere. Should each of
   them contact you separately?

Thank you for your work, and for any guidance you can give.

[name]
HumanityOS, https://united-humanity.us
