# The name "HumanityOS": what anyone may do with it

**Written 14 November 2026.** This is how the project asks its name to be used. It is not legal
advice, and nobody who wrote it is a lawyer. It does not claim the name is registered as a
trademark anywhere; it states the project's wishes and the reason for them, which hold either way.

## Why the name needs its own rules

The code, the Library and the data are free to copy and change ([`LICENSING.md`](../../LICENSING.md)).
None of those licences covers the name: among the terms the AGPL lets an author add, it lists
"Declining to grant rights under trademark law for use of some trade names, trademarks, or service
marks" (section 7(e)); CC BY-SA 4.0 says "Patent and trademark rights are not licensed under this
Public License" (section 2(b)(2)); and CC0 says "No trademark or patent rights held by Affirmer are
waived" (section 4(a)). The sources
and their dates are in
[`docs/reference/findings/2026-11-14-licence-change.md`](../reference/findings/2026-11-14-licence-change.md).

The reason is safety, not money. In HumanityOS your account is a key on your own device, backed up
by a 24-word recovery phrase, and whoever has that phrase has the account. A program that calls
itself HumanityOS and asks for your recovery phrase could take your identity and everything signed
with it. So the name has to mean one thing: the software this project publishes.

## What anyone may do, without asking

- **Talk about HumanityOS by name**: news, reviews, videos, teaching, comparisons, criticism.
- **Say what your work is built on**: "a fork of HumanityOS", "based on HumanityOS", "works with
  HumanityOS servers".
- **Say your server runs HumanityOS**, when it runs a release the project published, unchanged.
- **Link to the project**, with its name, and use its logo to point at the project itself.
- **Run a group, a meetup or an event about HumanityOS**, as long as it says plainly that it is
  run by you, not by the project.

## What the name may not be used for

- **A changed program, or a fork, presented as HumanityOS.** Change the code, and you choose a new
  name and a new icon for what you publish.
- **A name, an app listing, a website address or an account that people would take for the
  project's own**: "HumanityOS Official", "HumanityOS Pro", a lookalike of `united-humanity.us`.
- **Saying or suggesting that the project made, checked or endorses something it did not.**

## What a fork does

Forks are welcome; that is what the licences are for. A fork:

1. picks its own name and its own icon;
2. says plainly, where people download it and on its own About page, that it is not HumanityOS
   and is not made by the HumanityOS project (it may say it is based on HumanityOS);
3. keeps the licence notices, which the AGPL and CC BY-SA require anyway.

## Where the real HumanityOS comes from

- The website: <https://united-humanity.us>
- The releases on GitHub: <https://github.com/Shaostoul/Humanity/releases>
- The project's own copy of the code: <https://git.united-humanity.us/shaostoul/Humanity>

Type your recovery phrase only into a HumanityOS app you got from one of those places, never into
a message, a form someone sent you, or a program from anywhere else. If you find something
pretending to be HumanityOS, report it on the app's Bug Reports page or at
<https://github.com/Shaostoul/Humanity/issues>.

## The logo

The same rules cover the logo and the app icon. The image files themselves were released under
CC0 like the rest of the project's media, so copying the files is allowed; using them to pass
something off as HumanityOS is not.
