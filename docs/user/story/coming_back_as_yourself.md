# Coming Back as Yourself

*How death and waking work in the game's story, and why the person who wakes is the person who
died. The story is on the page [The Fleet](the_fleet.md); what is made up is on the page
[What Is Made Up in This Game](what_is_made_up_in_this_game.md).*

People have always asked the teleporter question. If a machine takes you apart here and builds a
perfect copy over there, did you travel, or did you die while someone new woke up? The copy would
say it was you. But your own view of the world would have ended.

The fleet never does that. It never copies anyone.

## One mind, two supports

When you joined the lifeline, your mind began to run in two places at once: on your brain, and on
the lifeline. Not a backup taken now and then: both at once, every moment, the way the two halves
of your brain carry one person now. Children who lose half their brain to surgery grow up as
themselves. One support was enough to carry them.

So when your body dies, nothing stops. Your mind loses one support and keeps running on the other.
It feels like the edge of sleep. A respawner grows a new body from the genes you were born with,
your mind settles into it, and you wake. You remember falling asleep. You remember everything.

It is still you for a plain reason: you are the pattern that keeps running, not the stuff it runs
on. The molecules in your brain are replaced again and again through your life, and you stay you.

## Three rules

Three rules keep it honest.

1. **Your view of the world never cuts out.** At death it dims, like falling asleep, and it comes
   back when you wake.
2. **There are never two of you.** A new body is grown only after the old one has died.
3. **The fleet never wakes a copy.** If a mind ever has no support left, that person has died, and
   the fleet will not make someone new and give them your name.

That last rule is why the lifeline's edge matters. Beyond it, your mind has only your brain, unless
your team takes a field lifeline with them: a heavy machine, carried like a backpack, that takes
the lifeline out there. Stay near it, and a death out there is only sleep too.

## What you keep, and what you lose

- **Everything you know.** Your skills, what you have learned, the quests you have done. That is
  your mind, and your mind never stopped.
- **Your look.** The new body is grown from your genes, with the look you chose at the mirror.
- **What you were wearing.** The fleet gives it back to you from its stores, so you never wake with
  nothing.
- **What you were carrying** depends on how hard your world pushes back (Settings, Gameplay,
  Death). In Simplified, the fleet brings it back with you. In Realistic, your backpack stays where
  you fell, in one pack, for an hour of play, so you can go back for it.
- **Anything added to your body** is gone, because it was not in your genes. A new body does not
  have it.

Every new body is made from the fleet's matter and energy, and the fleet writes it down, the way it
writes down every meal. Nobody is ever refused a body.

## Where you wake

Every home has its respawner: in a Homestead, a pod beside the main bedroom; in a cabin block, one
pod the block shares, beside its washroom. The ship has a public one too, in General Services, the
hall beside the Medical Bay, with bunks you can sleep in. Anyone can wake there, and it is where you
wake if you have no home of your own aboard.

When you die, the screen shows the places you can wake, your usual place first. To change your
usual place, walk up to a respawner and press E.

## The soul

The lifeline carries your mind. What else a person is, the fleet leaves to each person.

## The real ideas underneath

The lifeline is made up. The questions it answers are real, and people have thought about them for
a long time.

- **The teleporter question** is the philosopher Derek Parfit's, in his book *Reasons and Persons*
  (1984).
- **Changing slowly and staying yourself** is the old question of the ship of Theseus: a ship
  rebuilt plank by plank over the years is still the same ship.
- **Your brain's molecules are replaced.** In mice, brain proteins last about nine days on average
  (Price and others, *PNAS*, 2010). Most of your brain cells, though, last your whole life.
- **One half of a brain can carry a person.** Adults who had one half of their brain removed as
  children, to stop severe seizures, grow up as themselves (Kliemann and others, *Cell Reports*,
  2019).

Philosophers still disagree about what makes you you. The game does not need to win that argument.
It keeps one rule you can feel: the camera never cuts.
