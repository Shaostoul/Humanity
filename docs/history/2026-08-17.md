## v0.1155.0: tiled lights stay in their lane

The terrain night-glow fell to a one-line shader guard once the
mechanism was cornered: the tile lists are built for the lit interior
pass, but the terrain pass declares zero point lights, and only the
classic loop respected that declaration - the tiled path smuggled the
interior's lights onto the night terrain through the tile lists.
Tiled indices past the pass's declared light count are now skipped.
Probed at the operator's settings with tiled ON: dark night terrain,
dim rain, and seven extra fps from not evaluating phantom lights. The
night vantage keeps tiled on forever as the regression lock. Both
halves of the operator's night-glow report are closed.

## v0.1158.0: the physical cloud medium (phase 3)

A fidelity-expert analysis measured exactly why the deck still read as a
veil: the density chain discounted extinction a thousandfold, coverage
towered every column into a 4-8 km float, nothing in the pipeline could
make a cloud smaller than 11 km, and the High path never received
aerial perspective. Five targeted fixes - physical per-family
extinction with interior renormalization, a hard condensation base with
convection-keyed towering, the 8 km cell-split tap, aerial perspective
at the cloud's own marched depth, and a jitter decorrelation for the
moire rings - turned the from-below sky into a field of discrete
dark-based cumulus with a depth ramp to the horizon. The in-game
weather also owns the local sky now: a Cloudy or Rain HUD label
guarantees matching clouds regardless of what live MODIS says about
the real Silverdale. Remaining, fenced: temporal reprojection to
resolve the sampling grain into smooth cloud, then edge crispness.

## v0.1159.0: clouds stop being static (phase 4, temporal accumulation)

The operator named the残 failure precisely: "they look like static
instead of clouds." The fix is the production temporal-accumulation
pattern, bent to fit this engine without touching a single shared bind
group layout: a direction-indexed Lambert-azimuthal cloud map,
re-marched each frame with the animated jitter that used to BE the
static, EMA-blended so calm sky converges to a deep supersample while
rolling weather catches up fast. Camera rotation costs nothing by
construction - a direction does not change when the camera turns - and
the composite samples the map through the material's own albedo slot,
net FASTER than the march it replaced. The first probe used an
octahedral mapping and bilinear sampling smeared a band across its
fold; the Lambert map has no interior fold and the band vanished.
