# 2026-09-27: the ship's garden becomes a closed loop

Versions v0.1374.0 to v0.1378.0. The day started on a test-suite crash, turned
into the ship's life support after the operator chose the spaceship first, and
picked up a buzz the operator heard on every boot.

## A decoder that borrowed two columns too many (BUG-093, v0.1374.0)

`just verify` sometimes died outright: a rav1d worker thread panicked inside
its debug-only overlap checker, the panic could not unwind, and the whole lib
test process exited with STATUS_STACK_BUFFER_OVERRUN, hiding every other
result. The easy readings were both wrong. It was not our use of the decoder,
and it was not a false alarm that release builds could ignore.

rav1d 1.1.0's CDEF `padding` borrows the line buffer from two columns left of
each block, and at the frame's left edge it reads only from column 2. The two
columns it borrowed but never read are the end of the previous superblock row,
which another postfilter thread can be writing. No pixel was ever wrong (the
decode is byte-identical threaded and single-threaded), but a shared borrow of
memory another thread holds mutably is undefined behaviour in Rust, and a
release build keeps it with the checker compiled out. An earlier note had said
release was unaffected; that half was wrong.

The fix is `vendor/rav1d`, the published crate without the assembly our build
never compiles, with the borrow narrowed to the columns read. A new six-decoder
stress test aborted 8 runs in 20 on the published crate and none on the patched
one. Single-threaded decoding was rejected because 1080p needs the threads (69
fps against 28.4).

## A test that assumed a paused video stays still (BUG-094, v0.1374.1)

One screen test failed now and then under load. It paused a clip and expected
nothing new to draw, but pausing stops the clock and not the decoder, so a
decoder still behind the paused position kept delivering frames for it. The
player was right and the test was wrong. It now rewinds the paused clip to a
known place and waits until the decoder has got past the clock.

## Soil pH while away, and the tower pH (v0.1375.0)

Crops aged over the offline catch-up while the lime and sulfur in their soil
did not react at all. The farming tick now takes the time away, once, at the
player's own growth speed (which reaches the DataStore only after the resume).
The Home page's tower check had been intersecting the plants' SOIL pH windows
for a nutrient solution the model holds at 6.0; both shipped towers showed a
shared window of exactly 6.5. It now shows the setpoint.

## How long is a day?

Four clocks run the garden: the world (a 20-minute day), crop growth (10x on
top), the tanks (real days) and the drawn sky (the wall clock). Several loops
cannot balance until one is chosen. The question went into
`docs/design/decision-briefs.md` as Brief 6, with the recommendation to ship
both as the two modes (real days for full realism, 20-minute days for the
simplified mode). Still open.

## The buzz (BUG-095, v0.1376.0)

The operator: the screen "playing the constant high pitch buzz. That's very
distracting every single time a fresh instance boots up, especially when I'm
watching a movie or playing some other game." The workshop screen loops the
demo clip, whose sound is a 440 Hz test tone, and every boot started it,
including every instance an agent launched in the background. Video screens
now start muted with an Unmute button on the strip, and an instance launched
by a script opens no audio device at all, the same way it takes no focus.

## The ship's air and water (v0.1377.0)

A homestead-engineer agent built the closed loops. Air handlers condense the
water the crops breathe out and return it to the cistern: about 666 L a day in
the family home, all of it. Carbon dioxide from the people and the mushroom
tents is taken up by the crops, and a CO2 scrubber modelled on the ISS CDRA
runs only when they cannot keep up. Two modes: Station-supplied (the default)
and Realistic, in which neither home's energy budget closes yet. A tank-level
rounding bug found on the way (BUG-096) had been swallowing any flow under
about 0.9 L a minute.

A botanist agent sourced the mushroom yields, which had been a placeholder
about seven times too high, and counted hulled and shelled crops on what is
eaten. With honest yields the family home grows about 91% of its food and the
solo home about 81%.

## The garden batch (v0.1378.0)

Three worktree branches merged: whitefly, thrips and row covers; bumblebee
colonies that are bought and expire, a pollinating fan, and a "bearing" label
between picks; open mushroom shelving inside drawn tents, with the garden's
plant meshes built on a worker thread (a 1.8 s freeze at world entry gone). The
seam between them, found by the pests agent, was that a field under a row cover
still counted as pollinated by wild insects.
