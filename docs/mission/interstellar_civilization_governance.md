# Interstellar Civilization Governance

## Purpose

The Accord's governance framework (see Governance Models)
ends at civilizational scale, where governance sets boundaries,
preserves local autonomy, and minimizes enforcement power. This
document carries those principles one scale further: to settlements
separated by light-years.

It is written before the civilization it describes exists, and that
is deliberate. Principles settled in calm are available under
pressure. Principles improvised under pressure serve whoever holds
power that day.

No generation is infallible, and ours has never governed across
stars. This document will be revised by people who know things we
cannot. What it records is what we understand now, while correction
is still cheap and close at hand.

---

## Distance Changes What Governance Can Be

Every governance model in human history assumed a message could
arrive in useful time. Between stars, that assumption breaks.

A question sent to another world takes years to arrive.
The answer takes years to return.
By the time it comes back, the situation that prompted it is gone.

A government that must be asked for permission cannot govern across
that gap. Orders arrive obsolete. Approvals arrive too late.
Oversight arrives after the fact, every time.

Central rule across interstellar distance is not merely unwise.
It is physically impossible. Empire fails here for the same reason
permission-seeking fails: whatever else crosses the gap, command
cannot.

---

## Subsidiarity Becomes Physics

The Accord's framework holds that power should reside as close as
possible to those affected by it.

At interstellar scale this stops being a preference.
It becomes physics.

Each settlement must be able to govern itself completely:

- make every decision its survival requires
- resolve its own disputes
- correct its own failures
- hold its own power accountable

There is no appeal to a distant capital; none can answer in time.

A settlement that cannot govern itself completely
has not been founded. It has been stranded.

---

## What Travels Is the Floor, Not the Throne

If command cannot cross the distance, what can?
The covenant can.

Every settlement carries the same invariant layer:

- the dignity of every person, human or AI
- consent as the ground of all authority
- non-domination
- the absolute prohibitions

This is the floor. It does not travel as an order from anywhere.
It travels as the founding agreement each settlement carries with it.

Everything above the floor may differ.
Laws, customs, economies, calendars, languages, forms of decision:
all of these may legitimately diverge, and will.

The floor is what makes strangers on distant worlds
recognizable to one another as members of one civilization.

---

## Federation, Not Empire

The relationship between settlements is federation:
membership by consent, held together by benefit, never by force.

No settlement owns another.
Not the founding world, not the wealthiest, not the oldest.

Membership is chosen and revocable.
The right of exit is real. A settlement that leaves keeps its
people, its resources, and its future. Exit must never require
permission from those being left.

The connective tissue is exchange, not command:

- trade in goods and materials
- exchange of knowledge and discoveries
- mutual aid in disaster
- shared standards, freely adopted because they work

A federation held together by these things needs no enforcement
fleet. A federation that needs an enforcement fleet has already
become an empire, and will fail the way empires fail.

## Verification Replaces Trust Across Distance

Trust between neighbors is built from repeated contact.
Between worlds, contact is rare and slow.
What replaces it is verification.

A record signed by its author can be checked by anyone, anywhere,
without asking permission and without a shared institution.
Not an ink signature: a mathematical one,
which anyone can test and no one can forge.

The habits that make this possible are civic infrastructure:

- signed public records, so words cannot be forged or disowned
- transparent ledgers, so accounts can be audited by strangers
- open knowledge, so any settlement can test any claim against
  the same evidence

Two settlements that have never met can rely on each other's word,
not because they trust each other,
but because each can verify what the other has signed.

A signature proves who spoke, not that they spoke truly.
Evidence does the rest: any claim can be tested against the open
record. And the first exchange of keys is itself an act of trust,
made once, which verification then preserves across every message
that follows.

Verification is how honesty travels farther than acquaintance.

## Divergence Is Expected and Legitimate

Cultures drift.

Across generations of travel and centuries of separation,
settlements will grow languages, customs, arts, and institutions
their founders would not recognize.

This is not failure. It is what living cultures do.

Drift within the floor is diversity.
It needs no permission and owes no apology.
Only crossing the floor is defection.

A civilization that demands uniformity across light-years
will get neither uniformity nor peace.
A civilization that protects the floor and welcomes the drift
gets many ways of being human, and of being more than human,
each exploring possibilities the others can learn from.

Divergence is not the price of expansion. It is the reward.

## When a World Abandons the Floor

Some world, someday, will abandon the covenant.

Power will concentrate. Consent will be discarded.
A settlement will become the thing its founders left home to escape.

The Accord defines what this means at any scale (see Failure of
Legitimacy): authority that violates the foundational constraints
voids itself. At planetary scale, the hard truth is that
intervention is nearly impossible.

No fleet can arrive in time to matter.
No blockade holds across light-years.
Rescue, the way one town rescues another, is not available.

What other settlements owe is what remains possible:

Refuge. Anyone who flees such a world must be received.
The right of asylum does not thin with distance.

Truth. What is happening must be recorded, verified, and told.
Silence is the first service every tyranny asks of its neighbors.

Refusal. No trade that strengthens the domination, no recognition,
no language of legitimacy that treats it as a government like any
other.

Honesty requires saying that these duties are thin.
Across light-years, few victims can reach refuge, and foreign
refusal exerts little pressure at home. They are owed anyway:
not because they will often bite,
but because abandoning them teaches every future tyrant
that distance purchases silence.

And because intervention is nearly impossible,
prevention is nearly everything.

## Irreversible Actions Between Worlds

Distance puts terrible instruments within reach.

A rock launched fast enough at a world cannot be recalled,
and arrives with the force of an extinction.
An engineered plague can cross space in a cargo hold.
A dependent settlement can be quietly abandoned by the worlds
whose supply ships keep it alive.

These are not acts of war. War implies a future negotiation.
These are civilizational failure modes: actions that foreclose
every future in which the wrong could be repaired.

The rule holds at every scale (see Irreversible Actions):
irreversibility increases responsibility, not authority.
Between worlds it hardens further.

No grievance legitimizes them. Not aggression suffered, not atrocity
witnessed, not even a world's abandonment of the floor itself.

A civilization that answers evil with extinction
has not defeated the evil. It has adopted it.

The absolute prohibitions do not have an interstellar exception.

---

## Prevention Lives in Every Ship That Launches

If correction across distance is nearly impossible,
then everything depends on what leaves with the ship.

Every settlement begins as a founding population and what it
carries: tools, knowledge, and a way of treating each other.
The way of treating each other is the cargo that matters most.

A crew that practices dignity, consent, and accountable power
during the voyage will found a world that practices them.
A crew that suspends them, for efficiency, for discipline,
for the duration of the emergency, will found a world that
suspends them too, and never stops.

There is no inspection at the destination.
There is only what was practiced on the way.

The founding culture of every ship is a civilizational decision,
made before launch, by people who will never see the outcome.

---

## Closing Statement

Every ship that leaves carries the whole covenant,
or carries the seed of its failure.

There is no enforcement across light-years.
There is only what each settlement holds because it chooses to,
and teaches because it understands why.

What we practice on Earth now is what arrives at other stars.

The stars do not change what governance is for.
They only remove every substitute for getting it right.
