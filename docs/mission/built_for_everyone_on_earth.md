# Built for Everyone on Earth

## Purpose

There are about eight billion people on Earth.
HumanityOS is meant to work for every one of them:
to talk, share files, watch and stream, and organise,
without any company in the middle and without any one computer carrying everyone.

This page explains how that is possible, in plain words.
Some of it works today and some is still being built.
The technical version says which, part by part:
[eight-billion.md](../design/eight-billion.md).

---

## No Server Is Special

Most apps run on a company's servers.
When the company changes its rules, gets bought, or shuts down,
everyone's account and history go with it.

HumanityOS has one rule instead:
**no feature may need one particular server, or a big one.**

Everything has to work three ways:

1. **Device to device**, with no server at all.
   Two people in a room, or a village with no internet.
2. **Through any small server.**
   A community, a household, one person's computer.
3. **Across many servers** that talk to each other,
   each run by someone who chose to run it, none in charge of the rest.

A server is a meeting place and a mailbox.
It never owns anyone's identity or history.

---

## Your Identity Belongs to You

Your account is a key made on your own device from your recovery phrase.
No server creates it, and no server can take it away.

That means you can move.
If a server's rules stop suiting you,
you take your identity somewhere else, and everyone can still find you.

## Millions of Small Servers

Eight billion people on one server is impossible.
Eight billion people on millions of small servers is ordinary.

If one community server carries about a thousand active people,
everyone on Earth is about eight million servers,
each carrying only its own people.

The HumanityOS app on your computer already contains a server.
Anyone who installs it can run one for their household on their home network,
and making it just as easy to host a club or a town,
from settings inside the app with no command line, is part of the work ahead.

## Files That Get Cheaper as They Get Popular

On most services, a popular video costs its host more with every viewer.

HumanityOS is designed the other way round:

- **A file is cut into pieces**, each one checkable on arrival,
  so a download can continue from anyone who has the pieces.
- **Private files are sealed before they are cut**,
  so people who help carry them cannot read them.
- **A group shares a file among its members**,
  so a video sent to ten people costs the server one copy, not ten downloads.
- **People who watch something can help pass it on** if they choose to,
  so the more popular a file is, the more people are serving it.

Your network address stays private by default:
strangers' transfers go through a server rather than straight to your computer.

---

## Fair Shares When It Gets Busy

A server has only so much disk and bandwidth.
The person who runs it says how much,
and each person's share is worked out from that and from how many people are using it.

A quiet server gives generous shares.
A crowded one gives smaller ones.
Nobody can crowd anyone else out.

The server is a mailbox, not an archive.
Your files live on your own devices,
and a server holds them only while they travel.

---

## Giving Back

Running a server costs a little electricity and a little disk.
People who want to can offer some of theirs to their community,
and a ledger shows what each person used and contributed.

Nothing is paid, and nothing is hidden.
It is the same rule as everywhere else in HumanityOS:
contribute what you can, take what you need, and keep the record honest.
