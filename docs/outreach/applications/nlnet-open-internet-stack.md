## Decision, 2026-09-06: do not submit November 3

Verified against the live site on 2026-09-06, not from memory.

**The funds are named Restack and CodeSupply**, not a "Commons successor" or an
"Open Internet Stack" fund. Restack is the fit: it is the general build-the-open-
internet-stack programme with 7 million EUR to award through 2030, and its scope
names full stack security and trust enhancing technologies. CodeSupply is narrowly
about software packaging metadata and is not a fit. The Open Social Fund page
currently says it accepts no new proposals.

**The generative-AI policy is an eligibility risk, not a disclosure formality.**
This file previously described it as rules to follow exactly. That understated it.
Three escalating statements, all live:

- nlnet.nl/propose, in the general requirements: "We are not interested in
  AI-generated projects or proposals."
- The Restack FAQ, answering whether you may use generative AI to write part of a
  proposal: "The short answer is: no... Failure to [disclose] is likely to result
  in the proposal being rejected, and tarnishing your reputation."
- The call announcement of 2026-09-03: "the current policy already states that
  work which is (mostly) generated by LLMs ... is not eligible for a grant. In the
  next version of the policy this will be made more clear ... If you intend to make
  use of LLMs, you are advised to wait with submitting until the new policy is
  published in order to avoid investing time in a proposal that might not be
  eligible."

The revision will remove the exceptions the current policy allows, for new projects
specifically. It is not published yet.

**Why this lands on us specifically.** README.md line 166 states publicly that the
project is built by "one human (Shaostoul) and one AI (Claude), working together
daily", and 2,409 of 4,152 commits (58.0%, measured 2026-09-06) carry a
Co-Authored-By: Claude trailer. NLnet's stage-two review does independent
verification of claims, so a reviewer sees this immediately. Whether it reads as
"substantial human intellectual contribution" (permitted, with provenance) or as
"(mostly) generated by LLMs" (not eligible) is their judgement, and the burden of
showing the former is entirely ours. The honest position: the design contribution
is unambiguously the operator's, and the line-by-line code authorship is not.

**The decision.** Target January 3, 2027. The cost of waiting is small, because
decisions take three to five months from the deadline either way (November 3 means
roughly February to April 2027; January 3 means April to June). In exchange we
learn the actual rules instead of guessing at them, and we do not ignore the
funder's own written advice on the eligibility question right before they spend
three weeks interrogating our claims.

## Facts to build the eventual proposal on

- **A US applicant can apply**, but "European dimension" is a hard knock-out
  criterion checked before scoring, not a preference: "There are no categorical
  exclusions of persons who may not receive support from Restack", and applicants
  outside Horizon Europe countries must argue the European dimension affirmatively
  in the background field. The strongest move is naming a real European
  collaborator, contributor, or upstream relationship.
- **No legal entity or institutional affiliation is required.** An individual
  contracts and is paid directly. First proposal caps at 50,000 EUR.
- **Payment is milestone-based with nothing up front.** You divide the work into
  milestones, allocate an amount to each, and request payment as each completes.
- **They fund a scoped work package, not a platform.** Every comparable funded
  project is narrow and named: "implement draft-ietf-openpgp-pqc in Sequoia PGP",
  "add optimized post-quantum arithmetic". Closest precedents to us: Herbees
  (scalable intermediated P2P messaging on SimpleX), Katzen (metadata-minimising
  messaging over a mixnet), DID-SASL (DIDs and verifiable credentials as a SASL
  method).
- **Candidate work packages, all documented in this repo as deferred.** Preferred:
  the identity layer, meaning stable DIDs that survive key rotation (Phase 1.5,
  not built), friendship certificate expiry and revocation (deferred in the August
  privacy arc), and something that mints an identity anchor for an ordinary user
  (today only P2P group traffic writes to signed_objects, which is why the
  operator's own DID returns 404). Second choice: making federation genuinely
  multi-operator. Third: metadata protection, which our own privacy essay names as
  unbuilt and genuinely hard.
- **CC0 is FSF-recognised as free but is not OSI-approved.** NLnet requires outputs
  under "a recognised free and open source license". Dual-licensing the software
  under MIT or Apache-2.0 alongside CC0 removes an argument we should not have to
  have.
- The whole form is about 18,000 characters of prose across all fields, and they
  say it should take under an hour. Attachments are explicitly not needed.
