# Since the last public update

A running list of what has shipped since the last posts went out, kept so the
next update can be written from it without digging through the logs. The last
posts were the week update of 5 October 2026
(`docs/outreach/posts/2026-10-05-week-update.md`, v0.1421.1 to v0.1464.0).
When the next update is written, move this list into it and start a fresh one.

Each line is written the way a player would hear it; the release notes and
`docs/history/` hold the detail.

## Shipped

- **v0.1464.2: the server got its footing back.** A web crawler filled the server's disk on 5 October by asking the git mirror for over a hundred half-gigabyte copies of the code. Found and fixed on 9 October: the chat server is current again, the git mirror syncs again and now builds at most one downloadable copy a minute, the disk alarm posts once instead of every 20 minutes, and the server can no longer lose its own program while clearing space.
- **v0.1464.3: your data syncs only between your own devices.** Web chat's data sync (calendar, home records, notes, map pins) answered anyone who connected to you directly; now it answers only your other devices and asks before merging. And #announcements became one post per release: 5,325 bot notices became 2,354, each named by its release.
- **v0.1465.0: your network address stays yours.** Both apps now connect directly only to people you know (friends, your groups, your call); the web chat no longer starts a call before you press Accept; Google is asked for your address only when a call starts; pictures from other websites wait for a click; hiding your online status really hides it; nobody can add you to a group without you; and strangers' trade requests share the daily limit messages have.
- **v0.1466.0: friendships you can end, and that never run out.** Unfollowing someone now withdraws their pass at once, so they can no longer message you without limit; a friendship never expires on its own; and each pass says what a friend may do (message, voice message, invite, trade, call), with calls left for people you choose.
- **v0.1467.0: you choose who can reach you.** A new Safety section in Settings, in the desktop app and the web chat: for messages, calls and trades, choose Nobody, People I choose, Friends, Friends and people in my groups, or Anyone. Safe by default: messages and trades from friends, calls only from people you pick. A stranger can send a contact request that shows you only their name, and you accept or ignore it. The server enforces your choices for everyone, admins included.
- **v0.1468.0: Block.** In both apps, one click blocks someone: their messages, posts, calls, trades and requests disappear for you, the friendship pass you gave them is taken back, and they are not told. Your other devices learn it too. Settings > Safety lists everyone you blocked, with Unblock.
- **v0.1469.0: reports the admins can check.** Report someone with the messages they sent you as evidence; because every message carries its sender's signature, the server can prove they wrote it, and nobody can forge a report or pin it on someone else. Admins and moderators review reports in the app; the reported person is never told who reported them. For a child or anyone in danger, the dialog says to contact your local emergency number first.
- **v0.1470.0: calls that keep your address private.** Voice calls and voice rooms now go through the server, so nobody in a call sees another person's network address, and the apps no longer ask Google for anything. The forwarder can only pass audio between people in the same call or room. (Calls switch on once the server's port is opened.)
- **v0.1471.0: warnings that explain the trick, and a guard on your recovery phrase.** When someone who is not your friend sends a message asking for gift cards or a wire transfer, asking for your recovery phrase, pushing you to move the chat to another app, rushing you or telling you to keep it secret, or claiming to be staff, a short note under it explains the trick and what to do (the money and recovery-phrase notes show under friends' messages too, because an account can be taken over). Their links wait until you press Open. Nothing is read anywhere but on your own device, and nothing is reported. And neither app will send a message holding your own recovery phrase, even pasted as a numbered list.
- **v0.1472.0: choose exactly who may message, call or trade with you, and help outside the app when someone is in danger.** Settings > Safety now lists each friend once with a Message, Call and Trade tick, so "People I choose" works for all three, not only calls; a change takes effect on the server at once. And when you report that a child or someone else is in danger, the report dialog shows the emergency number and the official place to report a child being exploited online, for the country you pick (18 countries, each checked against the government or hotline's own page, and a directory of hotlines for the rest). The country stays on your device and is never sent.
- **v0.1473.0: the protected setup.** In Settings > Safety, a parent or carer can lock this device's safety settings with a PIN for someone they look after. Turning it on shows everyone who can already reach the device, so any of them can be removed. While it is on, only friends can message, adding a friend or joining a group or voice room needs the PIN, public rooms and pictures from people who are not friends are hidden, and a line on screen says it is on. Nothing about it is sent to any server. It does not check anyone's age, and we say plainly on screen what it cannot do. Also fixed: the desktop report dialog hid its Send button in v0.1472.0.
- **v0.1474.0: server admins can erase someone's data.** An admin or the server's owner can now delete everything their server stores about a member (messages, profile, uploads, membership, settings), for example when a parent asks or when an admin learns a member is a young child. They have to type the person's name to confirm, it works exactly like erasing your own account, and the person is told a server admin did it. It touches nothing on their own devices.
- **v0.1475.0: reports about a group go to the person who can act on them, and trading on the website works again.** A report about a message in a group can now go to the group's creator, whose app checks each reported message against its own copy of the group and can remove the person, changing the group's key so they cannot read what is said after. Also: when you are already in a call, another caller no longer learns you are online (it rings out and you see a missed call); if someone takes no contact requests, the app says so instead of offering the button again; requests other people sent you no longer stop you starting your own trades; and the website's Trade page, which had not been able to send a trade at all, signs in and works.
- **v0.1476.0: files in private conversations are as private as the words.** A picture or file you send in a direct message or a group is now encrypted on your device before it is uploaded, whether you attach it, paste it or drop it in (a pasted image in a web DM, and every file in a group, used to sit at a public address with only the link kept private). The desktop app now shows those pictures inline instead of a placeholder, and asks before showing one from someone who is not your friend. Also closed: pinning, editing or reacting in a private conversation could send its text to the server, and the desktop app wrote each DM file's key into its own log. And "seed" is gone from the account screens: it is your recovery phrase everywhere.
- **v0.1476.1 and v0.1477.0: a review of the whole week, and what it caught.** Three independent reviews read the server, the desktop app and the web chat side by side and found about thirty places they disagreed, all fixed with a test each: messages sent close together could be dropped (cutting a friend off); a reply could carry a private message into a public channel; a removed group member could rejoin with an old invite; reported staff could see reports about themselves; and the protected setup had several more ways around the PIN. One of the week's own fixes had stopped group conversations drawing in the web chat for a few hours; that was the first thing fixed.
- **v0.1478.0: a friendship is only counted once the server confirms it.** A second review, of those fixes, found that the server could turn away the message carrying a friendship pass without saying so, while the app showed the friendship as complete. Now the server answers each one, and the app counts a friend's pass only once the server says it took it; one that was turned away is sent again. Taking a permission away (unticking Call, say) still takes effect at once. Also fixed: someone removed from a group and later let back in now gets the group's key again.
- **v0.1479.0: the scratch pad keeps to itself on the website too.** A picture put in the web chat's scratch pad, which says it is local only, was uploaded as an ordinary public file. Now it is encrypted first and only your browser keeps the key, as the desktop app already did, and notes there no longer offer Edit or React, which would have sent their words to the server.
- **v0.1480.0 and v0.1481.0: blocking reaches the game, and calls you refuse never ring.** Someone you block is no longer drawn in the game and their name does not show over them. Your own device now checks who may call you as well as the server, so a call you refuse never rings. And other players are no longer sent your health, inventory or quest progress, only what they need to draw you: your name and how you look.
- **v0.1482.0: your choices hold across all your devices, and your desktop app no longer tells the server whose messages you open.** If you take a permission away from a friend on one device, your other device can no longer give it back. And the desktop app had been asking the server for a conversation's history when you opened a direct message, which put who you talk to into the server's request log; it no longer asks (the three log lines on our server age out by themselves within a day).
- **v0.1483.0: what each friend may do is the same on every device you use.** When you change what a friend may do (message, call, trade) on one device, your other devices now get that choice as a private signed note to yourself, and the newest choice wins everywhere. Your devices no longer try to work it out from the friendship passes themselves, which arrive late or out of order. And the website keeps your scratch pad notes encrypted, per identity, like the desktop app.
- **v0.1483.0 also: bigger uploads, and a chat that behaves.** Uploads now follow the limit a server's admins set for each role (up to 1 GB), instead of failing at 2 MB; a file that is too big is turned away straight away with the limit named. In the desktop app the picture viewer always opens on top, and a long message grows the typing box upward so you can read it back.
- **v0.1484.0: uploads are tied to your session.** A review of the new uploads found that an old shortcut let anyone upload as someone else who was online, by naming them; with bigger uploads that mattered more, so the bigger limit was paused within the hour, the shortcut was removed, and it came back once the fix was live. Photos sent from the desktop's Files page now lose their location data like any other, and the desktop app tells you why a file was turned away.
- **v0.1485.0: a Media page, big uploads that pick up where they stopped, and photos and videos that no longer tell where they were taken.** Both apps have a Media page for pictures, videos, 3D models and files, with categories, public or members-only items, labels that hide an item until you click, featured items and picks, and a storage meter for admins. Big files go up in pieces and carry on after a dropped connection. Phone videos and iPhone photos now have their location removed before anyone else can see them, as other pictures already did. Admins can set, per role, how big a Media or private file may be and how much someone can upload in a day.
- **v0.1486.0: pictures lose their hidden details before they leave your device, and profiles stop overwriting each other.** Both apps now strip where a photo was taken and the camera's serial number before anything is sent, with a switch to keep them. Saving your profile on the desktop no longer wipes the fields it does not show, and the website no longer undoes changes you made elsewhere. public.guide, the second server, talks to united-humanity.us again and now updates itself from each release.
- **v0.1487.0: big files go straight from one device to another.** A file too big for the server goes directly from the sender to the receiver in a direct message, between the desktop app and the website in any mix, without ever being stored on the server. Every piece is checked as it arrives, a dropped connection picks up where it stopped, and the connection passes through the server's own relay so neither person sees the other's address. "Accept files from" also stopped flipping when you switch servers.
- **v0.1487.1: the web chat fits a phone.** At phone width the header no longer runs off the edge, every button can be tapped, the text box fits, and uploads say how far along they are.
- **v0.1488.0: the groundwork for sharing your home.** The parts that will let you give a friend a household permit to walk into your home and build there are built and tested: a permit can be taken back at any time, Block takes back every permit you gave that person, and the server can say whether a permit works without storing anything. Long private messages are padded to the same size so their length gives nothing away, and public.guide's front page now explains how everything works, with a status on every part.
- **v0.1489.0: give a friend a key to your home, wounds that heal, and seeds that grow where you plant them.** A household permit, given from the Home page, lets a friend walk into your home on the shared ship and build there until the date you choose, and you can take it back at any time (proven in the running game, 27 of 27 checks). Your body now heals by itself when fed and rested, and bites and burns leave wounds with a course, shown on the HUD with what helps. A seed planted from the backpack goes into the nearest grow machine and says how long it takes in real time. On the website, Settings no longer asks for your camera and microphone when you open it, and the chat offers notifications once instead of asking on your first click.
- **v0.1490.0: a cabin or a homestead, a mess hall that feeds you, animals that need you, and profiles that reach linked servers.** The ship has a row of sixteen cabins (each the size of a standard single bedroom) beside First Street, and the server gives you the kind of home you ask for. Press E at the mess hall's serving counter for a free crew meal or to fill your bottles, and a second mess hall halfway down the street means every home is within five minutes of a meal. Farm animals now eat and drink real amounts and lay, milk and grow wool at real rates (a hen lays about an egg a day, not one every five minutes). The mining drone runs on your home's power and needs servicing. Your data folder now follows each update without touching files you edited. And your profile finally reaches the servers yours is linked with, signed by your own app, with anything you keep for friends left out.
- **v0.1491.0: each server keeps its own home for you, you choose where you live, and the Media page grows up.** Playing on a server now starts you fresh there, in its own save on your computer, while your own home stays exactly as you left it; only your name and look travel with you. In Characters you choose the home you start in (a Cabin, or the Homestead where most people start), and a server's card shows where you live there or lets you choose. On the website, the Media page is titled with the server you are on, sorts by newest, most picked or most downloaded, loads more as you scroll, shows a featured shelf and a description for each category, and gives every item its own address you can share; a strip under the top menu always says which server you are looking at. The server rules gained "Your uploads stay yours", and a way to report your own work posted without your permission.
- **v0.1492.0: the desktop Media page catches up, and shared links show what they point to.** The desktop app's Media page now names the server it is on, sorts, loads more as you scroll, shows each category's featured shelf and description, and has an Admin tab for the server's Media settings; a strip under the top menu always says which server you are on, with Switch. Downloads and views are counted, and a shared link to an item shows its title and description on social sites.
- **v0.1493.0: edit your Media items in the desktop app.** Click any item to open its own page (description, files, licence, counts, Copy link); Edit changes its title, description, category, labels, who can see it and its licence, and adds or removes files and a cover picture. The words saying your uploads stay yours now sit beside Add to Media.
- **v0.1494.0: edit your Media items on the website too.** The website now has each item's own page and the same Edit as the desktop app: words, category, labels, who can see it, the licence with its warnings, more files and a cover picture; Add to Media shows the licence choice and "Your uploads stay yours". In the desktop app, Open on a reported item goes straight to its page.
- **v0.1495.0: report your work if it was posted without your permission, and see what was taken down.** Every Media item, on the website and in the desktop app, has "Report a copyright problem": a short form anyone can send, signed in or not. Admins are told inside HumanityOS and decide in the Media page's Admin tab, and a public takedown log (on the rules page and in the app) shows what was removed or kept and why, never who asked.
- **v0.1495.1: the project's own history in the Library.** A new Project History shelf holds every dated session history and the monthly decision logs, so anyone can read how and why HumanityOS was built, inside the app.
- **v0.1496.0 to v0.1501.0: the chat's message box sits fully above the taskbar and shows every line you type.** The box no longer slips under the Windows taskbar, it keeps a small even gap around it, and as you type more lines it grows with them (up to a limit, then scrolls inside) instead of hiding the top lines while empty space grew below.
- **v0.1502.0: a window opened from another one comes in front of it.** In Media, Edit opened behind the item's page from the second time on, so it could not be used; now the window opened last is always in front, with the one beneath it dimmed. The same holds for every dialog and the picture viewer.
- **v0.1503.0: favorite folders, and the file picker opens where you left off.** Every file picker in the desktop app has "Add to favorites", so a folder like your models folder is one click away in all of them, and after you pick a file the next picker opens in that same folder.
- **v0.1504.0: choose several files at once.** Tick as many files as you like in the file picker. In Add to Media they make one item (the model and its pictures together), in an item's Edit window they are all added to it, and in chat each goes as its own message; the website does the same.
- **v0.1505.0: a mistyped recovery phrase is caught.** The desktop app used to restore a different, empty account when one of the 24 words was mistyped as another real word; now, like the website, it says one of the words is not right and to check each against your written copy.
- **v0.1506.0: your recovery phrase stays covered until you show it.** Everywhere the 24 words appear, on first run and after, they start covered, with a reminder not to show them while streaming or where people can see your screen, so nobody reveals them by accident on a livestream or in public. They cover themselves again when you switch windows. The QR that links a new device is covered the same way.
- **v0.1507.0: hold to show your recovery phrase.** Showing the 24 words now takes a 3-second press and hold, like the app's other hold-to-confirm buttons, so it can never happen by an accidental click.
- **v0.1508.0: look at 3D models in the app and on the website.** Any .glb or .stl file on a Media item now has View in 3D: turn it with the mouse, zoom in, see its real materials and textures under studio lighting. STL files for 3D printing are stood upright. Nothing downloads until you ask, and broken or huge files just say so.
- **v0.1509.0: videos and files posted in chat show properly, and a word about what a file is.** A video, song, document, archive or 3D model posted in a chat room is now a card with its name, size and Download in both apps, instead of a bare address; the website says when your browser cannot play a video, and a 3D model gets View in 3D in the desktop app. Uploaded files keep the words of their names. And from a suggestion in our Discord: under a file someone sends you, a line says when it is a program, a script, an archive or another kind worth care, and to scan it first; a file named like a picture or a video that is really a program is called out. The file picker also got a dimmed backdrop and colour-coded sections.
- **v0.1510.0: every file shows what it is, in a picture, a word and a colour.** Wherever a file appears in the app or on the website (chat, Media, the Files page, the file picker), a badge before its name shows its type with a small drawing and a word, green for pictures, videos, songs and documents, amber for archives and the like, red for programs and for any file pretending to be something it is not, so people who are new to computers, or who read little, can still tell. And the Media Edit window, Add to Media and the chat's "Send a file" are now in colour-coded parts, like the chat sidebar.
- **v0.1511.0: drag files onto the app, and post an upload straight into chat.** Drag files from your computer onto HumanityOS and it says where they will go before you let go. Dropped on a chat channel, they open the upload window with "Also post in chat" ticked: when the upload finishes, its title, description and file are posted in that channel (or any channel you pick, your favourites listed first). Dropped in a private conversation, they are sent privately and encrypted as always. The file picker gained List, Details and a Pictures view with real previews, one to eight across. And the warnings under risky files now say plainly what the danger is: stolen passwords, files locked until you pay, fake sign-in pages.
- **v0.1512.0: Media posts in chat are cards.** An upload posted in chat now shows as a card: its title, how many files and how big, opening out to its picture, description and every file it holds, each marked with its type. Click it to see the whole thing with a Download for each file. Small files now say their size in KB instead of "0 MB". In an upload's Edit window you can now rename any of its files (the ending stays, so a file never changes type), and a picture you added to it can become its cover with one button. File names can no longer hide what they are: invisible characters that flip text (which made a program called "photo...exe" look like a picture) are taken out wherever a name is shown, and long names keep their ending.
- **v0.1513.0: MP4 videos play in the desktop app, with sound.** An ordinary MP4, the kind a phone or a camera makes, now plays on the Media page and on the screens in the game world without converting it first: the app asks Windows to decode it, the way your other programs do, so we ship no video decoder of our own. Videos on the Media page now play with their sound, with a Mute button. On a copy of Windows without video decoders (an "N" edition) the app says so and names Microsoft's free fix. Also: Media cards in chat show content labels by their names, website replies in a thread get cards too, and sizes read the same on the app and the website.
- **v0.1514.0: play a video posted in chat, right there.** A video someone posts in a chat room now has a Play here button in the desktop app: it opens in its own window with its sound, Pause, Start again and Mute. MP4 videos included, on Windows. No more "download it to watch it".
- **v0.1515.0: videos show a picture in the file picker.** When you choose files to upload, the Pictures view now shows a frame from each video, not just its name, on Windows.
- **v0.1516.0: Your folders.** A new tab on the Media page in the desktop app: add the folders where you keep pictures and videos (a whole drive if you like), look through them in List, Details or Pictures view, tick the files you want, and press Put on Media. Files you already put on a server from this computer are marked with where they are, a link to them. The list of what you put where stays on your computer. On the website, a members-only item opened from a chat card now shows its picture and video and lets you download its files when your identity is protected by a passphrase.
- **v0.1517.0: a Games library.** A new Games page in the desktop app lists every game installed through Steam, the Epic Games Store and GOG, plus any game you add by its program, and Play starts it through the store's own app, the way a desktop shortcut does. Downloads and updates stay with each store (its button opens the store's app), nothing signs in to a store, and the page says why. On the website, Message Seller on a Market listing now opens an encrypted direct message with the seller, as the app does; the old message box there had stopped working. For developers and AI helpers: a one-line-per-file map of the code, and a test suite that runs faster.
- **v0.1517.1: the handbook every AI helper reads got shorter, and boot checks use the fast shader compiler.** The instructions every AI session and helper loads first went from 122 KB to 77 KB over two days with no rule lost (the long incident stories moved to the project history), and the quick boot check developers and AI helpers use now always compiles shaders with the fast compiler; without it a boot took ten minutes and then crashed (BUG-204, the crash itself is being fixed).
- **v0.1518.0: the app starts faster.** HumanityOS is ready to use in about 8.4 seconds instead of 13.3 on the test machine: the clouds' graphics programs, which took about nine seconds to prepare while everything else took three, are now prepared in the background and the clouds appear a moment later, and the planets are prepared while the menu shows. On a computer missing the fast shader compiler the app no longer crashes after ten minutes; it starts without clouds and says why.
- **v0.1518.1: four mistakes from the project's history can no longer repeat unnoticed.** Checks now catch, on their own, the four kinds of slip that once took the server down, crashed the game on entering the world, garbled source files, or put a release on the wrong version, each tested by making the original mistake again.
- **v0.1519.0: the Games page is a library now.** Your games show as a grid of covers or a list you can sort by name, last played, most played or size, and search. Click a game for its details: how long you have played it, how much space it takes, your achievements with the dates you earned them, your own screenshots, your Workshop mods, and links to its store page, PCGamingWiki and SteamDB. The covers come from the copies Steam already keeps on your computer, and nothing about your games leaves your computer.
- **v0.1520.0: the Tools page works like the Games page.** The free programs it recommends show as tiles or a list. The ones already on your computer are found and marked Installed, with a Launch button. Others can be installed from inside the app through Windows' own package manager: you first see the program's licence and what installing means, and nothing runs until you press I agree. Programs whose makers require their own website (DaVinci Resolve, for one) open that website instead, and the page says why. A line on the page explains where to get programs safely.

Library Genesis) and whose domains it ordered blocked. It came in with an old list copied over in July 2026 rather than by anyone's choice. In its place: Unpaywall, which finds the free legal copy of a research paper, arXiv and the Directory of Open Access Journals.
- **v0.1521.0: the Media page in three panes.** On a wide screen the list of everything is on the left, the item you pick fills the middle (a picture fitted to the space, a video, a 3D model, or a song with its waveform and a live spectrum you can click to jump through), and its details are on the right; on a tall screen the three stack top to bottom. Edit no longer opens a window on top: the details turn into the form, in coloured sections with a bright border so you can see you are editing, and Save puts them back. The old grid of tiles is still there as Gallery.
- **v0.1521.1: the website's Tools page looks like the app's.** Tiles or a list, search, sort and filters, a details panel, and the same advice on where to get programs safely; the website says plainly that only the desktop app can find, start and install programs on your computer.
- **v0.1522.0: the website's Media page in three panes too.** The same list, viewer and details as the app, stacked on a phone; editing in place in the coloured sections; a song's waveform and spectrum, with nothing of it downloaded until you press Play here.
- **v0.1522.1: the website's light theme works.** Choosing Light (or following your device's setting) now turns every page light, not just the top menu, with text dark enough to read easily; the Settings page's choice takes effect at once, and Midnight and OLED are offered there too.
- **v0.1523.0: Games and Tools are one Apps page.** Your games, HumanityOS's own tools, other free programs, help and services, and anything you add yourself, each in its own coloured part with its name on it. The tiles are smaller and show each name once. Programs installed through Steam link to Steam, DaVinci Resolve and other programs Windows has no record for now start from their Start-menu shortcut, and Epic games can show their covers if you switch that on (it asks Epic for the pictures, so it is off until you choose).
- **v0.1523.1: the website's Tools page is now its Apps page.** The same six coloured parts as the app, with HumanityOS's own tools opening their website pages and the games and your own additions pointing to the desktop app, which is the only one that can see your computer.
- **v0.1524.0: Media is now Files, and one page holds every file.** What people share on the server, the server's shared files, the app's own folder (its data, mods and assets, with text files you can edit and put back), and your own folders, as places on one Files page, in the app and on the website. Also fixed: a server's data files could be changed by anyone who reached it; now only its admins can, and only with a signed request.
- **v0.1525.0: four small things in the game work the way they should.** A drink puts back the water it actually holds (a cup is not a jug); a fire does the same damage and burns the same fuel whatever your frame rate; gasoline and diesel are real items the refinery makes from crude oil (and the generators run on gasoline); and the refinery's drum takes only the crude oil it refines.
- **v0.1526.0: tidier Apps and Files pages.** Each page's explanations fold behind one "How this page works" (the safety lines stay in view); programs you can install show Install right on their tile, still asking you to agree to the licence first; guides from the Library open in the Library's reader when you find them in Files; and programs installed from the Microsoft Store are found too.
- **v0.1526.1: the website shows only what it should.** The site's update step used to copy every data file in the server's folder to the public website, which could have published a file meant for members only (nothing had been, it was checked first); it now publishes only the game data in the project itself. And the pages retired along the way (Tools, Media, Shared Files, and an Agents page removed in August) are finally gone from the site instead of lingering.
- **v0.1527.0: food fills you by what it weighs, and falls hurt.** A loaf of bread no longer fills you for three days and a cucumber no longer waters you more than a jug: every food now feeds and waters you by its real weight, energy and water, from USDA's figures. Falling hurts by how hard you land, with the heights that sprain or break an ankle or a leg taken from injury research (gentler by default; the full numbers in Realistic), and ladders hold you instead of dropping you when you let go. There is a diesel generator to build now, and the refinery makes gasoline and diesel in the shares real crude oil gives.
