# HumanityOS for Funders and Sponsors

You have money you want to turn into good, and you have learned to be
careful. Most pitches that reach you oversell, hide their weak points, and
ask you to decide before you understand. This document tries to be the
opposite. It leads with the parts that usually get buried.

## Read this part first

There is nothing to invest in here in the traditional sense. There is no
company, no nonprofit, no equity, no revenue, and no exit. HumanityOS is
released into the public domain under CC0, which means nobody owns it, so
none of the usual instruments apply. You cannot buy a stake in it, and
neither can anyone else.

What exists instead is a chance to fund a public good directly, and to
watch every dollar become something everyone owns. If that is not what you
are looking for, we would rather you know now than be disappointed later.

## What HumanityOS is

HumanityOS is free software that teaches and supports self-sufficiency:
growing food, managing water and power, planning real projects, and
cooperating with the people nearby. The core idea is simple. Poverty is
needing permission to meet your own needs. A household that can feed
itself, repair its own shelter, and hold its own knowledge is hard to
starve and hard to coerce. We build the tools that grow that capability.

Where it stands today, honestly: the chat, tasks, maps, marketplace
listings, library, and identity system are real and live at
https://united-humanity.us. The game world that teaches these skills
through simulation is in active development and not fully playable yet.
Some of what is built is rough, and some will break. We say so on our own
mission page, because hiding it would betray the reason the project
exists.

## Why public domain is the point, not a bug

Tools meant to end poverty cannot be owned. The moment someone owns them,
they become the next gate: a subscription, a license, an acquisition, a
board that decides who gets access. The project's own analysis of how
poverty persists is that scarcity of access is profitable to whoever holds
the gate. Read the full argument here:
https://united-humanity.us/library#the-five-adversaries

CC0 removes the gate permanently. No one can capture HumanityOS, buy it,
fence it off, or sell it back to the people it was built for. An investor
cannot capture it, and that is exactly why a funder can trust it. Your
money cannot be redirected into someone's exit, because there is no exit.
Whatever your support builds stays free for everyone, forever.

## What funding actually buys

Three things, concretely:

- The maintainer's full-time work. One person builds this full time,
  alongside AI tooling, and funding is what makes that possible. This is
  by far the largest of the three.
- Infrastructure and tooling. The AI development subscription and the
  domains, DNS, email, and hosting bill. The books below itemize both.
- Pace. More support means faster development. Less means slower. The
  project continues either way.

The transparency is total because it has to be. Every line of code, every
change, and every decision is public. Roughly 1,000 releases have shipped
in the open so far, each one visible and verifiable at
https://github.com/Shaostoul/Humanity. You do not have to trust a report
we wrote about our progress. You can watch the work itself, any day, and
so can anyone you ask to check it for you.

## The books, itemized

Most projects say they are transparent. Here are the actual numbers,
as of August 2026, from the platform statements and receipts. When they
change, this section changes.

Income, per month:

- Sponsor-A-Can stipend: $600, steady since January 2024.
- Patreon: about $21 net over the last twelve months. (Twelve years of
  history there: $15,812 lifetime net since 2014, with a peak around
  $220 a month in 2019.)

Total: about $620 a month.

Costs, per month:

- AI development subscription (Anthropic): $200. The single largest
  cost, and the reason one maintainer can ship at the pace the public
  release history shows.
- Domains, DNS, email, and hosting (Namecheap): about $41, averaged
  over the last twelve months of receipts. That includes the roughly
  $20 a month server the live platform runs on.

Total: about $241 a month.

What remains is roughly $380 a month. That is what the maintainer lives
on while building this full time. It is livable because his family
provides a bedroom, internet, and most meals, and because he lives
frugally and does essentially nothing except build this. There is no
salary, no office, no travel budget, and no expense a funder would have
to wonder about. The underlying statements are available to any serious
funder who asks.

We publish this for a simple reason. This page is what one person
ships on almost nothing. What that person could do with real community
support is the question we are inviting you to ask.

## The mechanics, stated plainly

Support today is a personal gift to the maintainer. It is not a donation
to a charity, and it is not tax-deductible. There is no 501(c)(3), no
foundation, and no fiscal sponsor at this time.

If deductibility or formal grant structure matters for your situation,
talk to your advisor before giving anything. We would rather lose a gift
than surprise you. If your giving requires a structure that does not exist
yet, tell us what you need. Knowing what real funders require is itself
useful to us.

## What return looks like

There is no financial return. The return is capability that did not exist
before:

- people fed by skills they learned instead of aid they waited for
- households that can hold their own weight through a bad season
- communities that run their own infrastructure instead of renting it
- knowledge that compounds in public instead of expiring behind a paywall

These are the measures we hold ourselves to. The honest caveat, stated
outright: the platform has not launched, and the user numbers today are
effectively zero. What you would be funding is the trajectory, in the
open, where you can verify it.
The long-term return is a world with fewer desperate people in it, and
that return is paid to everyone, including you.

## What large support could unlock

No promises, only honest possibilities. Substantial support could mean:

- faster development, including additional paid contributors
- more languages, so the tools reach people the current five miss
- hardware for testing on the cheap, old, low-power devices real people
  actually own
- real-world pilot programs: taking the tools to actual households and
  communities and measuring what changes

Each of these is limited today by time and money, not by ideas.
