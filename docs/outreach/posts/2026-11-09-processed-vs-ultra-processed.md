# Processed vs ultra-processed food: the four groups (9 November 2026)

An educational post for X (a thread), Discord and Facebook, asked for by the operator on
2026-11-09 ("I don't feel like a lot of people know about this stuff"). Information, not medical
advice, and written without blame: ultra-processed food is often the cheapest and fastest food
there is.

Fact-checked on 2026-11-09 against the primary sources, each read that day:

- The four groups, their examples and the marker-ingredient test: Monteiro, Cannon, Lawrence,
  Costa Louzada and Pereira Machado, *Ultra-processed foods, diet quality, and health using the
  NOVA classification system*, FAO, Rome, 2019,
  <https://openknowledge.fao.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/5277b379-0acb-4d97-a6a3-602774104629/content>.
  Group 1 includes frozen vegetables, plain yoghurt, flour, pasteurized milk, tea and coffee; group
  3 lists "ham, bacon, pastrami, and smoked fish; most freshly baked breads; and simple cheeses";
  group 4 lists soft drinks, packaged snacks, "sausages, burgers, hot dogs", instant noodles and
  "mass produced packaged breads". The test: "The presence in the list of ingredients of one or
  more of these food substances identifies a product as ultra-processed", and the same for any
  additive of the listed classes (flavours, flavour enhancers, colours, emulsifiers, artificial
  sweeteners, thickeners and others). The report also lists baby formula in group 4; left out of
  the posts on purpose, since many families have no alternative and a short post cannot carry that.
- The health evidence: Lane et al., "Ultra-processed food exposure and adverse health outcomes:
  umbrella review of epidemiological meta-analyses", BMJ 2024, doi:10.1136/bmj-2023-077310 (read
  through Europe PMC, PMC10899807; bmj.com refused automated reading). Convincing evidence for
  cardiovascular disease deaths, type 2 diabetes, anxiety and common mental disorders; of 45 pooled
  analyses, 22 low, 19 very low and 4 moderate quality on GRADE.
- The trial: Hall et al., "Ultra-Processed Diets Cause Excess Calorie Intake and Weight Gain",
  Cell Metabolism 2019, doi:10.1016/j.cmet.2019.05.008: 20 adults at the NIH Clinical Center,
  508 kcal a day more on the ultra-processed diet, 0.9 kg gained in two weeks.

## Discord

**Processed vs ultra-processed: what's the difference?** 🥫

Most of us hear "ultra-processed" without anyone saying what it means. There's a standard answer, and you can check it yourself on any label.

Food scientists sort food into four groups, a system called **NOVA**, written up in a 2019 report for the UN's Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO):

**1. Unprocessed or minimally processed.** Fresh or frozen fruit and vegetables, eggs, plain meat and fish, milk, plain yoghurt, rice, beans, flour, nuts, tea, coffee. Frozen counts: freezing doesn't move a food out of group 1.
**2. Cooking ingredients.** Oil, butter, lard, sugar, salt.
**3. Processed.** A group 1 food with group 2 ingredients added: canned vegetables in brine, fruit in syrup, ham, bacon, smoked fish, simple cheese, fresh-baked bread.
**4. Ultra-processed.** Factory formulas, mostly from ingredients you'd never use at home: soft drinks, packaged snacks, sweetened cereals, nuggets, hot dogs, sausages, instant noodles, mass-produced packaged bread.

**How to tell on a label** 🔍
Read the ingredient list. Just **one** of these makes a food group 4:
- sugars like high-fructose corn syrup, maltodextrin, dextrose or fruit juice concentrate
- proteins like soy protein isolate, hydrolysed protein, whey protein or mechanically separated meat
- hydrogenated or interesterified oil
- any flavouring, flavour enhancer, colour, emulsifier, thickener or artificial sweetener

**Why it matters**
- A 2024 review in the BMJ pooled 45 analyses covering about 10 million people. It linked eating more ultra-processed food to deaths from heart disease, type 2 diabetes, anxiety and depression. Honest caveat: most of that evidence comes from watching people rather than experiments, and the review rated 41 of the 45 low or very low quality.
- The best single experiment: in 2019, 20 adults lived at the US National Institutes of Health clinic for a month, two weeks on each diet. On the ultra-processed diet they ate about 500 more calories a day and gained about 2 lb, even though both diets offered the same calories, sugar, salt and fibre.

**This isn't about guilt.** Ultra-processed food is often the cheapest and fastest food there is, and sometimes it's what makes a busy day possible. Knowing the groups just helps you choose. This is general information, not medical advice: for your own diet, talk to a doctor or dietitian.
