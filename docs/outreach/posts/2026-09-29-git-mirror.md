## Discord

**The code has a second home: git.united-humanity.us** 🪞

HumanityOS now keeps a full copy of its source code on our own server, running Forgejo (free, open-source software).

- Every commit and every version tag: nearly 4,800 commits and over 2,200 tags.
- Public: browse it or clone it, no account needed.
- It copies every change from GitHub by itself every few hours, so it can't quietly fall behind.

Why: HumanityOS is public domain (CC0). It should never depend on one company staying friendly. If GitHub ever went away, the code, and the whole history of how it was built, is still here.

In the spirit of full transparency: we had a mirror before, and it went down for eight weeks without anyone noticing after a server rebuild. This time it pulls from GitHub on its own, and our tools warn us if it falls behind.

Mirror: https://git.united-humanity.us/shaostoul/Humanity

## LinkedIn

I've never posted about HumanityOS here, so here's the short version of a project I've been building full time.

HumanityOS is a free, open-source, public-domain project with one goal: to help people provide for themselves and each other, so that fewer people live in poverty. It's made by a small open project, not a company. There's nothing to buy, and no sign-up: no email, no phone number, no password.

What exists today:

• A practical library. Step-by-step guides for people starting from zero: growing food, storing water safely, composting, heating water, batteries and home power. New guides get a second, independent check of every claim against its source before they go in.

• Private chat with no sign-up. Your account is a key created on your own device and backed up by 24 words only you hold, so no company holds it, not us, not anyone. Private messages are end-to-end encrypted with post-quantum cryptography (the NIST-standardized ML-KEM and ML-DSA), so nobody in the middle can read them, including us.

• A desktop simulation that teaches real skills. You live aboard a home spaceship and on the planets below it: grow crops that need real soil chemistry, pollination and light; build shelters that keep out wind, rain and a cold night sky; keep your body warm with a heat-balance model from thermal-comfort research. The numbers come from real agricultural, engineering and physiology sources, because what you learn in the game should hold up in real life.

Where it came from: on December 24, 2017, after coming home from emergency surgery and a week in intensive care, I dedicated my life to helping humanity however I best could. On January 28, 2019 I started building in public as Project Universe, and this year it became HumanityOS. Over 2,200 releases later, it's still one person building, with AI doing a lot of the typing, and every change is public.

This week's milestone is a quiet but important one. The project now keeps its own complete copy of its source code, on its own server, at git.united-humanity.us. Every change since the beginning is there, readable by anyone, and it updates itself from GitHub automatically. Public-domain software should never depend on one company staying friendly.

If any of this resonates, I'd love your help: try it, tell me what's confusing, share it with someone who'd care, or pitch in with code, writing or research. It runs on donations, and every one goes to development and hosting.
