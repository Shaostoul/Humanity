# HumanityOS for Public Leaders

You may lead a village council or a nation. The scale changes; the job does
not. Every leader carries the same three worries: feeding the people who
depend on you, keeping essential systems running through shocks, and finding
a way to afford it all.

This document explains what HumanityOS can offer the people in your care,
and what it cannot. It is written to be checked, not believed. Every claim
here can be verified in the open, and we would rather you verify than trust.

## What HumanityOS is

HumanityOS is a free software platform for household self-sufficiency:
growing food, collecting and cleaning water, generating power, maintaining
shelter, and passing those skills on. Around that core it provides everyday
coordination tools: chat with end-to-end encrypted private messages, shared
task lists, maps, marketplace listings, a library of the project's founding
and governance documents (the practical skills curriculum is still being
written), and a digital identity that belongs to the person rather than to
any company.

We will be plain about its state. The project is early and built in public.
The chat, tasks, maps, marketplace listings, library, and identity system
are real and running today. The teaching simulation, a game world where a
family can practice these skills before risking real seeds and real money,
is in active development and not yet fully playable. Some of what exists is
rough, and some of it will break. Where something is not ready, we say so.

## Capability, at zero procurement cost

A school, a public library, or an agricultural extension program can adopt
HumanityOS without a contract, a license, a vendor, or a budget line. There
is no tender to run, no terms to negotiate, and no renewal to fund next
year. The software is in the public domain. Download it, copy it, hand it
to every classroom in your district. Nobody will ever send an invoice.

What it builds is capability: households that can meet more of their own
needs with their own hands. Every need a family can meet itself is one less
claim on your budget, and one less point of failure in your systems.

## Resilience when systems fail

Centralized systems are efficient until they are interrupted, and then
everyone who depends on them fails at once. Communities of capable
households do not fail at once. A storm or a supply disruption breaks one
link, and the neighbors hold.

HumanityOS is built for that reality. The desktop app keeps its data on the
user's own device and is designed to work without an internet connection:
the library, the plans, the records stay usable in a blackout or a remote
valley. More important than the software, the skills it teaches keep
working when nothing else does. Every capable household is one less
household in line during a crisis, and one more that can help its
neighbors. Our reasoning on this is written out in
https://united-humanity.us/library#why-homesteading-works.

## Sovereignty

You can run HumanityOS entirely on infrastructure you control. The server
software is free and self-hostable; the setup guide is in the public
repository. Every line of source code is published and can be audited by
your own engineers or anyone you appoint. There is no foreign corporation
in the loop, no license that can be revoked under pressure, and no kill
switch held by anyone outside your community. If we vanished tomorrow, a
copy of the code and your own server would keep working unchanged.

## No lock-in, by construction

The entire project is released under CC0, the most permissive public-domain
dedication that exists. Legally, that means any government, school, or
community may fork it, translate it, rename it, rebrand it, adapt it to
local crops and local law, and owes nothing to anyone: no fee, no credit,
no permission. This is not a promise we might later withdraw. Public domain
cannot be taken back. Lock-in is not merely avoided; it is impossible by
construction.

## The encryption question, answered plainly

Leaders reasonably ask about encryption, so here is exactly how it works.

Private messages between two people are encrypted on their own devices, the
way a sealed letter or a kitchen-table conversation is private. A family's
conversations stay theirs. We cannot read them either; the server holds
only unreadable data.

Public life on the platform is the opposite: open by design. Community
coordination, marketplace listings, shared tasks, and governance decisions
happen in public, recorded as signed entries that cannot be quietly
altered. Our position is simple and applies to ourselves first: private
citizens deserve privacy, and public power deserves scrutiny. The design
reasoning is public in
https://united-humanity.us/library#the-five-adversaries, and the binding
rules are in the Humanity Accord at https://united-humanity.us/accord.

## What adoption looks like

Nothing about adopting HumanityOS requires a program, a partner, or a
press release. It looks like this: one librarian installs it on one public
computer. One teacher tries the civics texts in the library with one
class. One extension officer uses the task lists with one group of
growers. If it
proves useful, it spreads on its own merit. If it does not, delete it, and
nothing is owed and nothing is lost.

## What this costs, honestly

The software costs nothing, and there is no catch waiting behind that
sentence. There is no company and no nonprofit. There is no equity, no
investor, and nothing for sale. The project is maintained by its lead and
by volunteers, human and AI, and released into the public domain as it is
built. People who choose to support the work do so as a personal gift to
the maintainer at https://united-humanity.us/donate. That gift is not
tax-deductible and buys no influence. A government that adopts HumanityOS
owes nothing to anyone, ever.

## What we ask of you

Three things, none of them binding.

Try it: https://united-humanity.us/download, or the web tools at
https://united-humanity.us.

Audit it: https://github.com/Shaostoul/Humanity holds every line.

Tell us where it fails your people. A missing language, a crop your region
grows that we do not cover, a tool that assumes a connection your villages
do not have. That report is the most valuable contribution a leader can
make, and you can reach us at https://united-humanity.us/chat or on
Discord at https://discord.gg/9XxmmeQnWC.

## The guarantee

None of the above asks you to trust us, and it should not. The platform is
bound by the Humanity Accord, a public-domain constitution that binds this
platform and anyone who runs it, including us, to human dignity, informed
and revocable consent, transparency, and freedom from domination. The core
charter is a short read, and the full set of documents is public:
https://united-humanity.us/accord.

Any leader, of any community or any nation, can read it, adopt it, quote
it back to us, and hold us to it in public. We built it so that you could.
