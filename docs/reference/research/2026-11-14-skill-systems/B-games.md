# B. Games

How other games organise, level and reward skills. Research pass of 2026-11-14. Every
site's robots.txt was read in a fetch of its own before any page of that site. Quotations
are verbatim and under 15 words; everything else is paraphrase. Where the best page for a
game refused automated reading, the entry says so and uses a weaker source, and the
confidence line reflects that.

## B1. RuneScape / Old School RuneScape

- **Source:** "New Player Guide: What Do I Do in Old School RuneScape", Jagex (secure.runescape.com), https://secure.runescape.com/m=news/new-player-guide-what-do-i-do-in-old-school-runescape?oldschool=1 ; supporting: "Sailing Launches on November 19th!", Jagex, https://secure.runescape.com/m=news/sailing-launches-on-november-19th?oldschool=1 ; "Skills", RuneScape (RS3) game guide, https://www.runescape.com/game-guide/skills ; "Old School RuneScape", Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_School_RuneScape
- **Source's date:** New Player Guide 12 August 2025; Sailing post 24 October 2025; RS3 skills page no date on page (footer copyright 1999-2026); Wikipedia last edited 10 November 2026 ; **Read:** 2026-11-14
- **Quote:** "levelling these is the primary way that you'll progress your character." (Jagex, New Player Guide)
- **How skills are organised:** Old School has 23 skills and no overarching character level; each skill has its own uses and progression (Jagex guide). Wikipedia describes the skills as falling into broad kinds: combat, gathering, crafting and support. RuneScape 3 lists 29 skills, and its elite skill Invention needs level 80 in Smithing, Crafting and Divination before it can be started (prerequisite skills).
- **How a skill levels:** Purely by doing: each action tied to a skill gives experience, and the level rises as experience accumulates (RS3 guide, Wikipedia). Quests also hand out lump sums of XP or whole levels (e.g. early quests give Crafting, Runecraft or Fishing XP). In-game Tutors and a per-skill guide in the Skills tab show what the skill does and what each level opens. New skills keep the model: Sailing XP comes from successfully using boat facilities (trimming sails gives XP and a speed boost) and from four main activities (port tasks, salvaging, sea charting, time trials). No decay or loss. The exact XP needed for level 99 could not be confirmed from a page this pass was allowed to read (the community wiki that states it bars Claude's crawler).
- **What a level gives:** Mostly access: a level is a key. Examples from the Jagex guide: Lost City quest needs 31 Crafting and 36 Woodcutting; the Tempoross activity needs 35 Fishing; Wintertodt needs 50 Firemaking; Magic 37 opens teleports to three towns. Sailing crewmates unlock at Sailing 40. Some skills also give a passive effect (Agility improves run-energy regeneration and drain). RS3's page sums it up as new activities, equipment, locations and quests.
- **Fit for a game teaching the real skill:** Borrow the clear unlock ladder (every level shows exactly what it opens) and the in-game skill guide plus Tutors, and the idea that skills interlock (Sailing feeds and is fed by Construction, Fishing, Farming, Woodcutting, Crafting). Avoid the core loop of XP per repeated action: it rewards repetition, not understanding, and a level-99 grind teaches patience rather than the craft.
- **Confidence:** medium. Official Jagex pages confirm structure and unlock examples, but the community wiki (the source of the XP curve and the formal skill groupings) refused, so the curve number is absent and the grouping comes from Wikipedia.

## B2. The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim

- **Source:** "The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim", Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Elder_Scrolls_V:_Skyrim ; supporting: "Skyrim 1.9 Update - Patch Notes", GamingBolt (relaying Bethesda's patch notes, credited and linked to Bethesda's blog), https://gamingbolt.com/skyrim-1-9-update-patch-notes
- **Source's date:** Wikipedia last edited 10 November 2026, 23:47 UTC; GamingBolt published 9 April 2013 (patch 1.9 dated 18 March 2013 per Wikipedia) ; **Read:** 2026-11-14
- **Quote:** "which increases when the character performs an action in-game related to the skill" (Wikipedia, on each skill's experience bar)
- **How skills are organised:** 18 skills, split evenly across three schools: combat, magic and stealth (Wikipedia).
- **How a skill levels:** By use: each skill has its own experience bar that fills when you do something related (forging a dagger advances Smithing, hitting with a bow advances Archery); when the bar fills, the skill goes up a level and the bar resets. Once enough skill-ups accumulate, the character levels up. Patch 1.9 added Legendary skills: a skill that has reached 100 can be made Legendary, which drops it back to 15, gives its perks back, and lets it count toward character levelling again, which in effect removes the level cap (GamingBolt's relay of the patch notes; Wikipedia confirms the cap effect). No decay.
- **What a level gives:** A skill level makes the related action stronger; a character level lets you raise the maximum of health, magicka or stamina and gives one perk point, spent (now or later) on a perk belonging to one specific skill. Perks are the meaningful rewards: specialisation choices inside a skill.
- **Fit for a game teaching the real skill:** Borrow the two-layer model: practice raises the skill, and a separate, deliberate choice (the perk) decides what kind of practitioner you become, e.g. a cook who specialises in preserving versus baking. The Legendary reset is a useful idea for replaying a skill at a higher standard. Avoid pure use-counting, which players exploit by repeating trivial actions; in a teaching game the action counted should be a correct performance, not any performance.
- **Confidence:** medium. The structure is from Wikipedia (secondary) and the Legendary detail from a 2013 news relay of official notes; the primary community wiki (UESP) and the Fandom wiki both refused.

## B3. Wurm Online

- **Source:** "Skills", Wurmpedia (the game's community wiki), https://www.wurmpedia.com/index.php/Skills ; supporting: "Death", Wurmpedia, https://www.wurmpedia.com/index.php/Death
- **Source's date:** Skills page last edited 5 April 2025, 14:11; Death page last edited 15 November 2023 ; **Read:** 2026-11-14
- **Quote:** "Parent skills help the success chance of their sub-skills by a small amount."
- **How skills are organised:** A tree of parent skills and sub-skills (e.g. Archery with each bow type beneath it). Using a sub-skill also raises its parent, and the parent gives a small bonus to its children's success chance. Skills are also linked to characteristics (body and mind attributes) that rise slowly with the skill and in turn help the skills that use them. The page does not state a total skill count (the brief's "about 130" was not confirmed here).
- **How a skill levels:** By doing, with a chance per action. The chance of a gain depends on your current skill, the action's difficulty and usually the tool's quality; the amount scales with the action's timer (longer actions give proportionally more), with a ceiling of 1.00 skill per attempt. Improving an existing item gives much more than creating one, and some actions grant skill even on failure; gains are much lower when stamina is exhausted. A "sweet spot" doubles skill and attribute gain when you improve items well above your skill, given by the page as quality needed = skill x 0.77 + 23. On PvP servers nearby enemies reduce gain. Skills start at 1.0; without a premium subscription they are capped at 20. Death costs skill: 0.25 from five random skills plus 0.25 fighting (0.025 if you die inside your own settlement); skills above 99 are protected (except fighting and characteristics); lost skill is regained at three times the normal rate until you reach your previous best (except fighting).
- **What a level gives:** Better efficiency and success on actions that use the skill; titles at skill 50, 70, 90 and 100. (The page does not spell out a quality formula beyond the sweet-spot rule.)
- **Fit for a game teaching the real skill:** The closest existing model to real skill families: a parent/child tree (metalworking above blacksmithing, weaponsmithing) where practising a specialty also builds the general craft, and gain that depends on difficulty relative to your level is exactly the deliberate-practice zone (easy work teaches little, work slightly beyond you teaches most). Borrow both. Avoid the very long action timers and the subscription-style cap; mild death loss with fast regain is a reasonable "use it or lose it" softener, but not needed for teaching.
- **Confidence:** high for the mechanics stated (dated community wiki, specific numbers), medium overall because the skill count and the quality-of-result link were not on the pages read.

## B4. Vintage Story

- **Source:** "Classes", Vintage Story Wiki, https://wiki.vintagestory.at/Classes ; supporting: "Smithing", Vintage Story Wiki, https://wiki.vintagestory.at/Smithing ; "Survival Handbook" (wiki page of the in-game handbook's text), https://wiki.vintagestory.at/Handbook ; "Vintage Story", Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vintage_Story
- **Source's date:** Classes last edited 15 September 2026 (refers to game 1.21.6); Smithing last edited 1 October 2026 (verified for 1.20.6); Handbook page verified for 1.22.0, no edit date shown; Wikipedia last edited 22 October 2026 ; **Read:** 2026-11-14
- **Quote:** "the empty green outlines must be filled with metal voxels" (Smithing page)
- **How skills are organised:** There are no skills in the numeric sense. A character picks one of six classes (Commoner, Hunter, Malefactor, Clockmaker, Blackguard, Tailor), each a fixed bundle of bonuses and penalties (e.g. Blackguard: more melee damage, health and armour durability, but hungrier and worse at ranged and foraging) and, for some, exclusive recipes (Hunter bows, Tailor sewing). Nothing on the Classes or Smithing pages describes XP or levels.
- **How a skill levels:** It does not; the player gets better. Progression is knowledge and execution of procedures modelled on real ones: smithing means heating an ingot in a forge above half its melting point (iron bloom above 700 C), choosing the item on the anvil, then shaping a voxel grid (an ingot is 42 voxels) with hammer modes (heavy hit, upset, split), adding metal where green outlines show and removing it where purple ones show; quenching some tools improves them at the risk of destroying them. Clay molds are formed by placing clay voxels by hand (Wikipedia). The in-game handbook holds guides on food preservation, animal husbandry, prospecting, mechanical power, farming, steelmaking and more.
- **What a level gives:** Nothing numeric; the class traits are fixed at creation. What improves is what the player can do: knowing the steelmaking chain, executing the anvil pattern quickly and cleanly.
- **Fit for a game teaching the real skill:** The strongest model for teaching: the procedure itself is the gameplay, so learning the game is learning (a stylised version of) the craft, and the handbook is a built-in manual. Borrow the procedure-as-skill idea and the handbook. Its weakness is that mastery is invisible: no record, recognition or feedback that you have become good, which a teaching game needs (pair it with demonstration-based ranks, see B9).
- **Confidence:** high for what the pages state; the absence of XP is inferred from pages that describe progression without any XP, not from a page saying so explicitly.

## B5. Project Zomboid

- **Source:** "Project Zomboid Skills (Build 42)", pindrop.gg (community data site built from the dedicated server files), https://pindrop.gg/project-zomboid/skills ; supporting: "Project Zomboid", Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Zomboid
- **Source's date:** pindrop.gg page dated 6 November 2026, Build 42; Wikipedia last edited 13 November 2026, 14:56 UTC ; **Read:** 2026-11-14
- **Quote:** "24 skills have a five-volume book series." (pindrop.gg)
- **How skills are organised:** 35 skills in Build 42, grouped as 7 Melee, 2 Firearms, 12 Crafting, 5 Survival, 6 Physical and 3 Farming; each runs from level 0 to 10 (pindrop.gg).
- **How a skill levels:** By doing (actions and recipes), plus watching TV and VHS tapes and reading (Wikipedia, pindrop.gg). Per pindrop.gg most skills need 32,775 XP in total to reach level 10 (Fitness and Strength 487,500), with a rising per-level ladder. Skill books do not give XP; they multiply XP from practice, and only while your level is inside the volume's range: five volumes per skill, each covering two levels, at x3, x5, x8, x12 and x16 for 21 skills (lower for Long Blade, Aiming and Reloading). Starting levels from your occupation also scale gain (a skill you start with no levels in earns a quarter of the XP; three or more starting levels give x1.66). Traits: Fast Learner x1.3, Slow Learner x0.7. Occupations grant starting levels (Carpenter +4 Carpentry, Chef +4 Cooking). The site also notes its stored values are 1.5 times the game's registered values, so treat the XP totals as approximate.
- **What a level gives:** The page lists the recipes each skill governs (114 for Carpentry) but does not state the per-level effects, so this pass could not confirm the brief's "carpentry unlocks recipes and raises results" from a readable source.
- **Fit for a game teaching the real skill:** Borrow the book model: study does not replace practice but makes practice count for more, and only at the level the book is written for. That matches real learning closely and maps directly onto an in-app Library: reading the right guide multiplies what the next attempts teach. Occupations as prior experience (a real carpenter starts ahead) also fits a game where the player's real-life skills could be recognised. Avoid making reading a pure multiplier with no content; in a teaching game the book should be the real guide.
- **Confidence:** medium. The numbers come from a community site derived from game files (dated, specific); the official blog and the community wiki both refused.

## B6. Kenshi

- **Source:** "Kenshi (video game)", Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenshi_(video_game) ; supporting: "My Kenshi Training Guide from Zero to Hero - Part 1", GOG.com forum, player-written by user wpegg, https://www.gog.com/forum/kenshi/my_kenshi_training_guide_from_zero_to_hero_part_1
- **Source's date:** Wikipedia last edited 19 September 2026, 04:26 UTC; GOG post 22 August 2020 ; **Read:** 2026-11-14
- **Quote:** "Skills are leveled up through doing actions" (Wikipedia)
- **How skills are organised:** No character levels or classes; the player starts with no skills and builds them through play (Wikipedia). Stats cover combat, thievery, labour and crafting; the exact categories and counts were not on a readable page.
- **How a skill levels:** By doing: stealing raises thievery, fighting raises combat skills (Wikipedia). The player guide describes the folk practice: strength rises from carrying loads (walking encumbered gives roughly half the gain), toughness rises from being knocked down and getting up while enemies are near (early gains of 4 or 5 points, tied to how many enemies), defence from blocking against groups, and being outnumbered speeds gains. The brief's "faster when the opponent is stronger" multiplier is documented on a community wiki page ("Stronger Opponent Logic") that returned not-found to this reader, so its numbers are not given here. Injuries cripple body parts with skill penalties (Wikipedia).
- **What a level gives:** Per the player guide: strength improves speed, toughness decides how long you last before bleeding out, and higher smithing and armour-making skill unlock masterwork output with fewer failures. No formulas on the pages read.
- **Fit for a game teaching the real skill:** Borrow the principle that hard practice teaches more than easy practice, and that skill has bodily consequences (an injured hand works worse). Avoid what grew around it: players deliberately getting beaten up or overloading characters to farm stats, which is exploit-shaped training that teaches nothing real.
- **Confidence:** low to medium. Wikipedia is thin and the detail comes from one player's 2020 guide; the community wikis (Fandom and kenshi.wiki) could not be read.

## B7. Mortal Online 2

- **Source:** "Skills guide" by Crowax, Mortal Online Info (mortalonlinemap.info), https://www.mortalonlinemap.info/guides/31 ; supporting: "Patch Notes - Reckoning Expansion", Star Vault (official), https://www.mortalonline2.com/patch-notes-reckoning-expansion
- **Source's date:** guide: no date on page, and it does not say whether it covers Mortal Online 1 or 2; patch notes 16 July 2025 ; **Read:** 2026-11-14
- **Quote:** "Each character has a skill limit of 1100 primary skill points" (guide)
- **How skills are organised:** Primary skills count against a shared cap; secondary skills are free and unlimited. Child skills need a minimum level in their parent before they can be learned, training a child also raises the parent, and a child's usable level can never exceed the parent's (guide). Four clades (Human, Alvarin, Oghmir, Thursar) each have a distinct benefit tied to an unlockable Clade Gift (official patch notes).
- **How a skill levels:** Through use for skills marked trainable by action; others must first be learned from a book, sold by librarians and special vendors, which usually trains the skill to 70; reading takes time and continues offline (guide). The cap is 1100 primary points (attribute bonuses add some, about 1200 usable); at the cap you mark skills to fall (-) or to lock, and raising one skill lowers another, so breadth costs depth. Combat abilities need a minimum weapon skill (official: 50 Sword for Flurry, 100 Sword for Hanging Thrust) and are taught by consumable Combat Treatise books found in specific towns.
- **What a level gives:** Access to abilities and child skills at thresholds, and stronger results in the skill; the cap forces each character to specialise in roughly 11 skills at 100.
- **Fit for a game teaching the real skill:** Two ideas to borrow: books (theory) bring you to a competent base and only practice takes you to mastery, which is how real trades work; and child skills that can never exceed their parent (no specialist without the fundamentals). The hard cap forces interdependence between players, which suits a cooperative game, but forgetting a skill you mastered to make room for another is a poor fit for real-life skills, which people keep.
- **Confidence:** low to medium. The detailed guide does not confirm which game version it describes; the official forum threads refused; only the patch notes are official.

## B8. EVE Online

- **Source:** "Skills and learning", EVE University Wiki, https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Skills_and_learning ; supporting: "Ship Tree", EVE University Wiki, https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Ship_Tree
- **Source's date:** Skills and learning last edited 19 April 2026; Ship Tree last edited 19 November 2022 ; **Read:** 2026-11-14
- **Quote:** "allows you to train skills in real time, even when logged off" (Skills and learning)
- **How skills are organised:** 494 skills as of 1 March 2025, in 24 skill groups; free (Alpha) characters are limited to 175 skills. Many skills have prerequisites (other skills at given levels).
- **How a skill levels:** By time, not by doing. You buy and inject a skillbook (which adds the skill at level 0 and destroys the book), queue it, and it accumulates skill points in real time, online or off; when one skill finishes the next in the queue starts. Each skill has five levels; for a rank 1 skill the cumulative skill points are 250, 1,414, 8,000, 45,255 and 256,000, and a skill's rank (1 to 16) multiplies that requirement (a rank 16 Titan skill needs 4,096,000 points for level V). Speed comes from five attributes (each skill has a primary and a secondary), boosted by implants (+1 to +5) and occasional remaps; a default paid character gains about 30 points a minute, a free one half that. Unallocated skill points can be applied directly.
- **What a level gives:** Each level grants a fixed bonus and opens prerequisites for ships, modules and further skills. Ship Mastery levels I to V are curated lists of skills recommended for a hull; completing a level is shown as a tick and suggests the next, as guidance with no in-game bonus mentioned (Ship Tree page).
- **Fit for a game teaching the real skill:** Avoid the core mechanic: real-time training while offline separates the skill from doing it, the opposite of what a teaching game needs. Borrow the Mastery lists: a named, levelled checklist of what a competent person in a role knows ("Homestead electrician, level II") works as a curriculum and maps onto real certification ladders.
- **Confidence:** high for the numbers (a well-maintained, dated wiki); the official support article refused, so queue-size and paid/free rules from CCP itself were not confirmed.

## B9. Certification by demonstrated task (Starfield) and one perk model (Fallout 4)

- **Source:** "Starfield (video game)", Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starfield_(video_game) ; "All Starfield skills: Categories, tiers & skill trees explained", GGRecon, https://www.ggrecon.com/guides/starfield-all-skills-categories-tiers/ ; "Starfield skills, perks, challenges" (Kotaku), https://kotaku.com/starfield-skills-perks-challenges-best-starter-1850820087 ; perk model: "Fallout 4", Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallout_4
- **Source's date:** Starfield Wikipedia last edited 14 November 2026, 08:40 UTC; GGRecon 5 September 2023; Kotaku 11 September 2023; Fallout 4 Wikipedia last edited 8 November 2026, 21:32 UTC ; **Read:** 2026-11-14
- **Quote:** "Each skill can be ranked up by completing related challenges." (Wikipedia, Starfield)
- **How skills are organised:** Starfield: 82 skills in five trees (Physical, Social, Combat, Science, Tech), each with four tiers; a deeper tier opens after enough points in the tree (tier 2 at 3 points, tier 3 at 7, tier 4 at 11, per GGRecon). A character background grants three starting skills (Wikipedia). Fallout 4: seven S.P.E.C.I.A.L. attributes, and more points in an attribute open more perks tied to it, about 275 perks in all (Wikipedia).
- **How a skill levels:** Starfield splits it in two. Character levels come from general XP and give one skill point each (GGRecon); a point buys rank 1 of any skill, but each later rank (four in all) needs that rank's challenge completed first and then another point (Kotaku). Challenges only start counting once the skill is bought. Examples: Weight Lifting asks you to run carrying 75 percent of your capacity; Wellness, using healing items; Security, picking a number of locks (Kotaku). GGRecon notes level 328 would be needed to max every skill (no level cap). Fallout 4: level from XP, soft level cap 65535 (Wikipedia).
- **What a level gives:** Each Starfield rank gives a stronger bonus in that skill's area. Fallout 4 perks unlock abilities and improve efficiency, and can themselves be upgraded.
- **Fit for a game teaching the real skill:** The challenge gate is the most useful structure in this survey for a teaching game: a rank is earned by demonstrating the thing, not by accumulating time. Borrow it, but make the challenge a real competence check (wire a circuit that passes inspection, splint a fracture correctly, forge a blade without cracks) instead of a count (N kills, N metres), which Starfield's examples mostly are and which reduces to grinding. Fallout's attribute-gated perk chart adds little for real skills.
- **Confidence:** medium. Wikipedia confirms the challenge mechanic; detail comes from two 2023 launch-period guides; Bethesda's own support pages forbid automated reading.

## B10. Simple-model contrast: Valheim and Stardew Valley

- **Source:** "Valheim", Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valheim ; "Stardew Valley", Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stardew_Valley
- **Source's date:** Valheim last edited 12 November 2026, 05:39 UTC; Stardew Valley last edited 7 November 2026, 21:06 UTC ; **Read:** 2026-11-14
- **Quote:** "Each skill can be increased up to a level of 100" (Wikipedia, Valheim)
- **How skills are organised:** Valheim: a flat list of skills, each to level 100, each tied to one mechanic (spear damage, stamina drain while sprinting). Stardew Valley: five skills: farming, foraging, fishing, mining and combat.
- **How a skill levels:** Not described on the pages read; the brief's "by use, lose some on death" (Valheim) and "profession choice at levels 5 and 10" (Stardew) could not be confirmed, because the Stardew community wiki bars Claude's crawler and the Valheim Fandom wiki was not attempted (Fandom refused every robots.txt fetch this pass).
- **What a level gives:** Valheim: a better number in the one mechanic the skill governs. Stardew: not stated on the page read.
- **Fit for a game teaching the real skill:** A small, flat list of everyday skills is easy to understand and is a useful default ("simplified mode") over a deep tree. On its own it says nothing about how well the player can actually do the task.
- **Confidence:** low. Both pages are thin on mechanics; included only as a contrast.
