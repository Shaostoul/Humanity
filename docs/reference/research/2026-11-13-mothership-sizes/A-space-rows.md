# Helper notes A: Community and support spaces per person (README section 1)

Part of the [mothership sizes research](README.md) (2026-11-13). A research helper wrote these
notes on 2026-11-12 from the sources it could read; the README carries the answers and the
judgements. After the helper finished, every cited host's robots.txt was checked: findings
read from a page that refuses Claude's readers are marked REMOVED and their numbers are not used.
Quotations were spot-checked against their sources where the README says so; the rest are the
helper's readings, with the helper's own confidence. The helper's own robots notes and summary
table were written before that check: where they name a REMOVED finding or say a host allowed
reading, the check below the README's "How this was read" section is the one that holds.


Method note: I read what I could. Where a page refused (403) or a PDF would not read, I used the figure the search tool quoted and mark it "paraphrase, no exact quotation, read via search summary" with lower confidence. Per-person ship figures below are my arithmetic from the cited numbers; the assumptions are stated.

## Findings

### A1. Hospital beds per 1,000 people (OECD average)
- The number(s): about 4.3 beds per 1,000 people (2021 OECD average); 4.4 (2019); Japan and Korea about 12.5 to 12.6 per 1,000 at the top of the range.
- Source: Health at a Glance 2023 (OECD), via search summary; OECD pages returned 403 to me. https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/health-at-a-glance-2023_7a7afb35-en/full-report/component-33 , source's own date: 2023
- Quotation: paraphrase, no exact quotation (page could not be read).
- Read: 2026-11-12
- Confidence: medium (OECD is authoritative, but I only saw the search summary).
- Use for the ship: 4 to 5 beds per 1,000 is the modern developed-country norm. SP-413's 5 per 1,000 (50 per 10,000) agrees.

### A2. World Bank hospital beds series
- The number(s): no value readable on the page; the indicator covers 1960 to 2023.
- Source: Hospital beds (per 1,000 people), SH.MED.BEDS.ZS, World Bank, https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SH.MED.BEDS.ZS , source's own date: data through 2023
- Quotation: "WHO data, supplemented by country data"
- Read: 2026-11-12
- Confidence: high on provenance (WHO data), no number obtained.
- Use for the ship: confirms the bed series is WHO data. Our World in Data (https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/hospital-beds-per-1000-people) also gave no values in the page text; its description reads: "Hospital beds include inpatient beds available in public, private, general, and specialized hospitals".

### A3. Hospital floor area per bed (whole hospital)
- The number(s): 90 m2 per bed before 1990, rising to 135 to 155 m2 per bed now in the USA and France. UK examples roughly 85 to 120 m2 per bed and a French example about 107 m2 (search summary of the same report, not read from the table; low confidence for those).
- Source: Learning from French Hospital Design, BDP (architects), 05/04, https://www.gov.je/freedom of information library/idfoi bdpfrenchhosp 3-2 - ref email 1 20190122.pdf , source's own date: May 2004
- Quotation: "Whole hospital area per bed is rising from 90m2 pre 1990 towards the 135–155m2 current in the USA and France."
- Read: 2026-11-12
- Confidence: medium (one architect's report, 2004, but it states the figure plainly; US rule-of-thumb articles in the search summary run 120 to 260 m2 per bed, so the range is wide).
- Use for the ship: 58 m2 a bed (SP-413) is the ward area only; whole-hospital gross is 135 to 155 m2. Result: 4.3 beds per 1,000 x 135 to 155 m2 = 0.58 to 0.67 m2 per person (5 per 1,000 gives 0.68 to 0.78). Recommend 0.6 m2/person for a full hospital, about twice SP-413's 0.3. If the sector uses single-bed rooms (the French model, 18 m2 per bed room), the higher end applies. A young, screened crew could justify 0.4 as a lean option.

### A4. UK school floor area per pupil (BB103)
- The number(s): minimum gross area = base + per pupil: primary 350 m2 + 4.1 m2 per pupil; secondary (to 16, up to 750 pupils) 1,050 m2 + 6.3 m2 per pupil; post-16 7.0 m2 per pupil; maximum gross primary 4.5, secondary 7.1. Worked: 420-pupil primary = (350 + 4.1x420)/420 = 4.9 m2 per pupil; 750-pupil 11-16 secondary = 7.7 m2 per pupil. These include halls, dining and PE (0.3 to 0.6 per pupil), learning resources, staff, storage, and circulation and plant. Dining allows 0.9 m2 per place.
- Source: Area Guidelines for Mainstream Schools (Building Bulletin 103), UK Department for Education, Annex A, https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5f23ec238fa8f57acac33720/BB103_Area_Guidelines_for_Mainstream_Schools.pdf , source's own date: June 2014
- Quotation: "Generally, the gross area of new buildings will vary between 142% and 145% of the net area in primary schools" (table row as extracted: "Minimum gross area 350 700 1050 1400 4.1 6.3 7")
- Read: 2026-11-12
- Confidence: high (national guidance, read from the PDF).
- Use for the ship: use about 5 m2 per primary pupil and 7.5 m2 per secondary pupil, blended 6 to 6.5 m2 per school-age pupil.

### A5. Share of population that is school age
- The number(s): UN DESA says school-age children (age range not stated in the snippet) fall from about 51% of the total in sub-Saharan Africa as the highest, to 21% in Europe and Northern America as the lowest. Hmm: the search summary read "51%" and "21%" as regional shares; I cannot confirm what the percentages measure, so treat this as unusable. The world school-age population was nearly 1.7 billion in 2022.
- Source: UN DESA Population Division expert group presentation, https://www.un.org/development/desa/pd/sites/www.un.org.development.desa.pd/files/undesa_pd_2022_egm3_presentation_danangu.pdf , source's own date: 2022
- Quotation: paraphrase, no exact quotation (search summary only).
- Read: 2026-11-12
- Confidence: low (I did not get the clean percentage).
- Use for the ship: no clean number found. Use 17 to 20% aged 5 to 17 as a planning assumption (my own, flagged as unsourced). 18% x 6.5 m2 = 1.2 m2 per resident. SP-413's 1.0 agrees within reason; recommend 1.2 if the sector is family-heavy, 1.0 otherwise. Needs the UN WPP 5-17 series to be firm.

### A6. Public library floor per capita
- The number(s): historic rule 0.5 sq ft per capita (0.046 m2); then 0.6, 0.75, and by 2000 about 1 sq ft or more; many planners are back at 0.75 sq ft per capita (0.07 m2). Texas applied 0.6 minimum; Delaware required 0.75 for state aid.
- Source: Omaha Public Library Facilities Master Plan Update, Executive Summary, Himmel & Wilson, Library Consultants, https://omahalibrary.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/24/2017/07/2017-Omaha-Facilities-Master-Plan-Update-Exec-Summary-7-5-17.pdf , source's own date: July 5, 2017
- Quotation: "many have now returned to the 0.75 SF/capita level"
- Read: 2026-11-12
- Confidence: medium (a consultant's study summarising state standards, not the state documents themselves).
- Use for the ship: 0.07 to 0.1 m2 per person. SP-413 folds the library into Schools ("the Library hall"); a sector of 500 needs only about 40 to 50 m2 of public library, so keep it inside the school/learning allowance and add 0.1 m2/person for lending, study and computer space.

### A7. Retail floor per person
- The number(s): US 23 sq ft per person (2.1 m2); UK and France 5 sq ft (0.46 m2); Spain 4; Germany 2 (0.19 m2); Canada 17 (1.6 m2). A search summary also gave Japan mall space about 0.41 m2 per capita. UK Employment Density Guide: high street retail 19 m2 per worker.
- Source: PaymentsJournal (Raymond Pucci), citing the International Council of Shopping Centers, https://www.paymentsjournal.com/u-s-still-faces-a-glut-of-retail-space/ , source's own date: August 6, 2019
- Quotation: "According to the trade group, the U.S. has 23 square feet of retail space per person."; "In contrast, the U.K. and France each have five square feet per person."
- Read: 2026-11-12
- Confidence: low to medium (a trade press story quoting ICSC; ICSC's own table not read; one outlet warns such comparisons have measurement problems).
- Use for the ship: SP-413's 2.3 matches the US, which is the world's highest and widely called oversupplied. Europe sits near 0.2 to 0.5. No source found for market halls or eating-out floor per capita. Recommend 1.0 m2 per person for shops plus markets plus food (my judgement: between the European and US figures, because a closed ship has no outside trade).

### A8. Swimming and sports halls per 1,000 people (Sport England model)
- The number(s): England average swimming water space about 12 to 12.9 m2 of water per 1,000 people (0.012 m2 per person); sports halls about 4.2 badminton-court equivalents per 10,000 people (2018 and 2020 reports). Using my own assumption of about 145 m2 of hall per court (a 4-court hall of 33 x 18 m, not sourced here) that is about 0.06 m2 per person.
- Source: Sport England Facilities Planning Model local reports (Durham 2018, Shropshire 2019 to 2020, Harlow 2011), e.g. https://www.shropshire.gov.uk/committee-services/documents/s26041/Appendix 5 - Shropshire County Swimming Pools Report.pdf ; the PDF would not read as text, so the numbers are from the search summary. Source's own date: 2011 to 2020 (varies)
- Quotation: paraphrase, no exact quotation. (Search summary quoted: "for England wide it is 4.2 courts per 10,000 population in 2018".)
- Read: 2026-11-12
- Confidence: medium for the order of magnitude, low for exact values.
- Use for the ship: formal sport halls plus pools are only about 0.1 m2 per person (pool hall roughly 2 to 3 times the water area). SP-413's Recreation 1.0 must be mostly gyms, lounges, play areas, hobby rooms. No fitness-centre floor-per-member figure was found. Recommend keeping 1.0 as a generous social-recreation allowance and splitting it in the room sheet: 0.15 sports hall and pool, 0.35 gym and studios, 0.5 lounges, play, games.

### A9. Outdoor sport and play (Fields in Trust) and US parks
- The number(s): Fields in Trust benchmark 2.4 ha per 1,000 people (24 m2 per person), split 1.6 ha outdoor sport (of which 1.15 ha pitch sports) and 0.8 ha children's play. US typical agency: 10 acres of parkland per 1,000 residents (40.5 m2 per person).
- Source: Fields in Trust, Guidance for Outdoor Sport and Play: Beyond the Six Acre Standard (Nov 2020), figures via Torfaen SPG and search summary https://www.torfaen.gov.uk/en/Related-Documents/Forward-Planning/SPG-Annex-6-Recreation-and-Public-Open-Space.pdf ; NRPA, https://www.nrpa.org/publications-research/ParkMetrics/ , source's own date: undated page
- Quotation (NRPA): "with 10 acres of parkland per 1,000 residents."
- Read: 2026-11-12
- Confidence: medium (UK split read via a council document summary; NRPA page gives no year or "median").
- Use for the ship: both are much larger than 10 m2. The drum's whole floor is the land, so 10 m2 is a floor, not a typical figure.

### A10. China's urban planning code GB 50137-2011 (per-capita land and shares)
- The number(s): total urban development land 85.1 to 105.0 m2 per person (grade 2); 105.1 to 115.0 (grade 3). Shares of land by category (numbers read; the Chinese labels were lost in text extraction, so I matched them to the code's well-known order and they must be checked): residential 25.0 to 40.0%; public management and services 5.0 to 8.0%; industrial 15.0 to 30.0%; roads and transport 10.0 to 25.0%; green space and squares 10.0 to 15.0%. Single-category per-capita values: residential 28.0 to 38.0 (or 23.0 to 36.0); public management and services not more than 5.5 m2; roads and transport not less than 12.0 m2; green space and squares not less than 10.0 m2 per person, of which park land not less than 8.0 m2. No separate logistics share appeared in the extracted text.
- Source: Code for classification of urban land use and planning standards of development land, GB 50137-2011, section 4.2 to 4.4, Ministry of Housing and Urban-Rural Development (English version on ChinaUP.info copy), https://gaizaoban.nwu.edu.cn/chengshiyongdifenleiheguihuajiansheyongdibiaozhun.pdf , source's own date: 2011, effective 1 Jan 2012
- Quotation: "85.1 ~ 105.0 m2/" (as printed in the extract; the person unit was lost)
- Read: 2026-11-12
- Confidence: medium (primary standard, numbers read directly, but category labels inferred).
- Use for the ship: the 10 m2 per person for green space and squares is a national minimum, so the ship's 10 is a defensible floor. Industrial land 15 to 30% of 85 to 105 m2 is 13 to 31 m2 of LAND per person, not floor; at a plot ratio of 0.5 this is 6 to 16 m2 of floor, so heavy industry 6.0 is at the low end, fitting a ship with no export economy.

### A12. Employment density (UK, workshops, offices, industry, warehousing)
- The number(s): floor area per full-time worker: general office 12 m2 (NIA); light industry 47 m2 (NIA); general industrial 36 m2 (GIA, range 18 to 60); warehouse and distribution 70 m2 (GEA, range 25 to 115); large high-bay warehouse 80 m2 (GEA); high-street retail 19 m2 (NIA); restaurants and cafes 18 m2 (NIA, range 10 to 30). Rows read from a garbled text extraction; values are consistent with the guide as I know it but check the table.
- Source: Employment Densities Guide, 2nd Edition, Drivers Jonas Deloitte for the Homes and Communities Agency, https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a7dedd8e5274a2e8ab44baf/employ-den.pdf , source's own date: 2010 (a 3rd edition, Nov 2015, exists; I did not read it)
- Quotation: "Ratios are generally expressed as the number of square metres per employee."
- Read: 2026-11-12
- Confidence: medium (official guide, 2nd edition, table read from a poor extraction).
- Use for the ship: workshops and service industry: 36 to 47 m2 per worker x 10% of residents = 3.6 to 4.7; SP-413's 4.0 agrees. Offices: 12 m2 NIA (about 17 m2 gross with 1.4 circulation) x 6 to 8% of residents = 0.7 to 1.4; SP-413's 1.0 agrees. Heavy industry: 36 m2 per worker x 15% of residents = 5.4, so 6.0 agrees. Warehousing: 70 m2 per worker x 1.5% of residents = 1.0; agrees. The resident-share percentages are my assumptions and must be set by the ship's job plan.

### A13. Emergency shelter floor (Sphere)
- The number(s): 3.5 m2 of covered living space per person in warm settings (excluding cooking); 4.5 to 5.5 m2 per person in cold or urban settings where cooking and sanitation space are included. Called a "key indicator", not a hard law.
- Source: Sphere Handbook shelter and settlement standard, via search summaries of a shelter analysis and UNHCR/ECDC guidance; spherestandards.org and emergency.unhcr.org both returned 403, so the handbook was NOT read.
- Quotation: paraphrase, no exact quotation. (Search summary: "4.5–5.5 square metres of living space per person in cold climates or urban settings".)
- Read: 2026-11-12
- Confidence: medium (the 3.5 m2 figure is very widely reported; I could not read the primary text).
- Use for the ship: a shelter in a sealed, climate-controlled drum is "cold or urban", so use 4.5 m2 per bed. 600 m2 per 2,500 people = 0.24 m2 per person shelters about 133 people at 4.5 m2 (5.3% of the sector population). Keep 600 m2; it is a sensible reserve.

### A16. Hostel and shared-house bathrooms
- The number(s): many English councils require one bathroom suite and one WC per 5 sharing occupants (Cheltenham, Stoke); Wandsworth requires 2 bathrooms and 2 WCs for 5.
- Source: Cheltenham Borough Council, Amenity and room size standards, https://www.cheltenham.gov.uk/download/downloads/id/9721/amenity_and_room_size_standards.pdf (the page 404 when I tried to read it; figure is from the search summary); Stoke-on-Trent https://www.stoke.gov.uk/download/downloads/id/1765/hmo_amenity_standards.pdf (not read)
- Quotation: paraphrase, no exact quotation.
- Read: 2026-11-12
- Confidence: low to medium (several councils agree per the summary; none read).
- Use for the ship: a shared bathroom per 5 people at about 4 m2 each gives 0.8 m2 per person; adding laundry and lockers brings it near 2.0. Keep 2 m2 per person.

### A17. Net to gross and wall share
- The number(s): gross is 140 to 145% of net in UK secondary schools and 142 to 145% in primary, up to 150% in existing buildings; the non-net area covers circulation, plant, toilets, kitchen and internal walls.
- Source: BB103 (see A4).
- Quotation: "the gross area of new buildings will vary between 142% and 145% of the net area in primary schools"
- Read: 2026-11-12
- Confidence: high for schools; not tested for other building types.
- Use for the ship: BB103's 1.4 to 1.45 already includes walls and plant. The ship adds 1.4 circulation AND 10% structure, about 1.54 combined, which is above BB103's new-build range and at the existing-building ceiling. Recommend a combined 1.45 to 1.5 (1.4 circulation and 5% structure) or keep 10% and treat it as slightly generous. No source for columns and shafts as a separate share.
