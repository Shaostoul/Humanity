# OpenMoji: may we host and show its emoji, what must we credit, and what does it mean for our own set?

**Researched: 2026-11-20.** Not legal advice: this was written by developers reading primary
sources, not by lawyers.

**The question.** May HumanityOS serve OpenMoji's emoji pictures from its own servers and draw them
in both apps, what exactly must it show to do so, and what does OpenMoji's licence mean for the
volunteer-drawn HumanityOS emoji that will replace them one at a time?

## The short answer

- **Yes, as they are.** OpenMoji's graphics are licensed under CC BY-SA 4.0, which allows copying
  and redistributing them, money included, with credit. Showing the unchanged pictures inside our
  chat does not put the chat (or anything else of ours) under CC BY-SA: Creative Commons says the
  licence of a collection is the collector's choice and share-alike reaches only the licensed
  material.
- **The credit OpenMoji asks for**, word for word: "All emojis designed by OpenMoji – the open-source
  emoji and icon project. License: CC BY-SA 4.0" (linking openmoji.org and the licence). Its README
  names a project README, an "About" section or a footer as the usual places. We show it on the
  desktop's Credits page, in LICENSES.md, CREDITS.md and LICENSING.md (one click from every web
  page's footer), and in a LICENSE.md beside the pictures.
- **Changing a picture makes an adaptation**, which must be shared under CC BY-SA 4.0 (or a licence
  Creative Commons lists as compatible), credited, saying what changed. So a volunteer's emoji can
  be public domain (CC0, as `docs/outreach/volunteers-wanted.md` offers) only if it is drawn from
  scratch; one traced from, or edited out of, an OpenMoji file stays CC BY-SA 4.0.
- **OpenMoji's code is LGPL-3.0**, a different licence; we use none of it (only the pictures, and
  our own matching code).

## Findings

### 1. OpenMoji's licence and requested attribution (its README)

- **Source:** <https://github.com/hfg-gmuend/openmoji/blob/master/README.md> (the repository's
  front page shows the same README). Read 2026-11-20 on the `master` branch; the page shows no date
  for the README itself. The release we copied is 17.0.0, whose files are dated 2025-07-17 inside
  the zip.
- **The licence of the graphics** (the README's License section, footnote 1): "the Creative Commons
  Share Alike License 4.0 (CC BY-SA 4.0)". The front page's version: "OpenMoji graphics are
  licensed under the Creative Commons Share Alike License 4.0".
- **The code is separate**: "Code licensed under the GNU Lesser General Public License v3".
- **The attribution section** is headed "Attribution Requirements" and says "Please give us
  credits!" Its suggested credit, as plain text, with its link targets
  <https://openmoji.org/> and <https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/#>: "All emojis
  designed by OpenMoji – the open-source emoji and icon project. License: CC BY-SA 4.0". The dash
  is an en dash (U+2013).
- **Where**, as the page extraction returned it (cut at its end by the reading tool): "Common places
  for attribution are for example: to mention us in your project README, the 'About' section or
  the footer..."

### 2. The release file itself carries no licence

- **Source:** `openmoji-svg-color.zip`, OpenMoji 17.0.0, from
  <https://github.com/hfg-gmuend/openmoji/releases> (downloaded by the orchestrator 2026-11-20 with
  the operator's yes; SHA-256 `59b0cd9f6fe033818fc02585cea42bea9fba5d68a4d3a3639bfe5d5cc3805689`).
- **What it holds:** 4,495 `.svg` files and nothing else: no LICENSE, no notice, no README. So the
  licence and the credit must travel in a file of our own beside the pictures
  (`assets/emoji/openmoji/LICENSE.md`).
- Every picture holds only shape elements (svg, g, rect, line, polyline, path, circle, polygon,
  ellipse, defs, clipPath): no script, no link, no reference out (checked by
  `scripts/lib/emoji-pack.js` `checkSvg`). Two files (2B1B.svg, E06C.svg) carry a garbled German
  word in an `id` attribute; we repair it and say so (`SOURCE.md`).

### 3. What CC BY-SA 4.0 asks (the legal code)

- **Source:** <https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/legalcode.en>, "Version 4.0" (the
  page dates its corrections on a separate errata page). Read 2026-11-20.
- **The credit's parts**, Section 3(a)(1)(A), to be retained "if it is supplied by the Licensor":
  "identification of the creator(s) of the Licensed Material", "a copyright notice;", "a notice that
  refers to this Public License;", "a notice that refers to the disclaimer of warranties;", "a URI or
  hyperlink to the Licensed Material to the extent reasonably practicable;". OpenMoji supplies a
  creator and a licence link (finding 1); no copyright notice comes with the files (finding 2).
- **Changes**, Section 3(a)(1)(B): "indicate if You modified the Licensed Material and retain an
  indication of any previous modifications; and".
- **Where it may be shown**, Section 3(a)(2): "You may satisfy the conditions in Section 3(a)(1) in
  any reasonable manner based on the medium", and its example begins "For example, it may be
  reasonable to satisfy the conditions by providing a URI or hyperlink to a resource that includes"
  (the required information).
- **Share-alike**, Section 3(b): "if You Share Adapted Material You produce, the following
  conditions also apply." Condition 1: "The Adapter’s License You apply must be a Creative Commons
  license with the same License Elements". Condition 2: "You must include the text of, or the URI or
  hyperlink to, the Adapter's License You apply."
- **What an adaptation is**, Section 1: "Adapted Material means material subject to Copyright and
  Similar Rights that is derived from or based upon" the licensed material (the definition goes on
  to translating, altering, arranging, transforming or otherwise modifying it).

### 4. Collections and adaptations (Creative Commons' FAQ)

- **Source:** <https://creativecommons.org/faq/>, the page stamped 2025-05-13. Read 2026-11-20.
- "An adaptation is a work based on one or more pre-existing works."
- On a collection that includes CC-licensed work: "this does not change the license applicable to
  the original material." And: "CC licenses do not require the collection or the compilation itself
  to be made available under an SA license."
- On combining works where no adaptation is made: "you may combine any CC-licensed content so long
  as you provide attribution."
- On a changed picture (a photograph in the FAQ's example): "the modified photo would need to be
  licensed under the same terms."

## What is still unknown, and what would settle it

1. **Whether the 17.0.0 release notes add any condition** beyond the README. Read
   <https://github.com/hfg-gmuend/openmoji/releases/tag/17.0.0> (free, minutes). Nothing suggests
   they do: the licence is stated for the folders the release is built from.
2. **Where "inspired by" ends for a volunteer's drawing.** Copyright protects a drawing's
   expression, not the idea of a smiling face, but no rule draws the line for us. Settled in
   practice by the volunteer rule below (start from a blank canvas, never from or over an OpenMoji
   file); settled in law only by a lawyer, if a specific design is ever questioned.
3. **Whether the website should show the credit inside the chat itself** rather than one click away
   through the footer. Section 3(a)(2) allows a link to a page holding the credit, and OpenMoji's
   README lists a footer as a usual place, so the footer route meets the words; a line in the
   emoji or reaction picker would meet them more plainly. A design choice, not a legal question.

## Sources I could not reach

- **<https://openmoji.org/>** and its pages (the library, the FAQ): its robots.txt (read
  2026-11-20) disallows ClaudeBot from every path, with a comment pointing AI crawlers to the GitHub
  repository instead, so no page of the site was read. The README on GitHub is the primary source
  used. Hoped for: any attribution or licence wording on the site that differs from the README.

## What it means for this project

**Certain** (from the quotations above):

- We may serve OpenMoji's pictures from our own servers and draw them, unchanged, in the website's
  chat and later in the desktop app, provided the credit, the licence link and a note of our changes
  travel with them. That is now the case: `assets/emoji/openmoji/LICENSE.md` and `SOURCE.md`, the
  `openmoji` row of `data/credits.ron` (the Credits page), LICENSES.md, CREDITS.md, and LICENSING.md
  (linked from every web page's footer).
- The chat, the website and the app stay under their own licences: putting unchanged pictures in
  them is a collection, not an adaptation (finding 4).
- A changed OpenMoji picture is CC BY-SA 4.0 and must say what changed (findings 3 and 4).
- None of OpenMoji's LGPL-3.0 code is used; `web/shared/emoji.js` and the index are ours.

**Judgement calls** (accept the facts above and weigh these yourself):

- **The two repaired `id` attributes** change no drawing; they are probably not a "modification" in
  the copyright sense at all, but we name them anyway, which costs nothing.
- **The volunteer set** (`docs/outreach/volunteers-wanted.md`, "Emoji designers"): each design is
  drawn from scratch, never traced from, started from or edited out of an OpenMoji file, so it can
  be offered under CC0 as the page says. A design that does start from OpenMoji is still welcome but
  is CC BY-SA 4.0, credited to OpenMoji and to its drawer, and the set's folder must then say so for
  that picture. Because the sets are separate folders (`assets/emoji/sets.json`, the first set
  holding an emoji draws it), each picture's licence follows its folder.
- **The index** (`assets/emoji/index.json`) lists file names made of Unicode code points; we judge
  that a list of code points is not OpenMoji's creative expression and treat it as our own data.

Not legal advice. Where this summary and the licence text disagree, the licence governs.
