# What do the building rules ask of a private home's inside stair, its guards and its ceiling heights?

**Research date: 20 October 2026.** Everything below was read on that day. If you
are reading this later, check each source's own date against anything that has
changed since: the International Code Council publishes a new International
Residential Code every three years (the 2024 edition has already renumbered the
stair sections), US states adopt editions on their own timetables and amend
them, and England's Approved Documents are under review by the Building Safety
Regulator.

This is a reading of public sources by an AI working on the project, not legal
advice. Nobody who wrote it is a lawyer, an architect, a building official or a
building inspector. The people who decide how these rules apply to a real house
are the local building official (in the US) and the building control body (in
England), working from the edition their jurisdiction has adopted.

## The question

For a private dwelling's internal stair and upper floor, what do the US
International Residential Code and England's Approved Document K (with Approved
Document M and the nationally described space standard where they bear on it)
say about the largest riser, the smallest tread (going), the steepest pitch, the
headroom over a stair, the stair's width, handrails (height and where required),
guards (height, where a drop requires one, and the largest opening allowed in
one), and the lowest ceiling a habitable room may have? And does the game's
existing staircase, and the planned 3.0 m floor-to-floor with a 0.25 m slab,
meet them? (OSHA's rules for workplaces are added for work platforms only.)

## Short answer

The numbers, side by side. "IRC" is the 2021 International Residential Code as
reproduced by the sources in findings 1 to 3; "AD K" is Approved Document K,
2013 edition, for a private stair in a dwelling.

| Rule | IRC 2021 | England, AD K 2013 | Stricter of the two |
|---|---|---|---|
| Riser | at most 7 3/4 in (196 mm) | 150 to 220 mm (5.9 to 8.7 in) | 196 mm (7.72 in) |
| Tread / going | at least 10 in (254 mm); a nosing of 3/4 to 1 1/4 in (19 to 32 mm) unless the tread is at least 11 in (279 mm) | 220 to 300 mm (8.7 to 11.8 in) | 254 mm (10 in), or 279 mm (11 in) for a stair drawn with no nosing |
| Rise and going together | (no rule) | 2R + G between 550 and 700 mm (21.7 to 27.6 in) | 550 to 700 mm |
| Pitch | no stated limit (196 over 254 is 37.7 degrees) | at most 42 degrees | 42 degrees, and the riser and tread limits above |
| Rise of one flight | at most 12 ft 7 in (3835 mm) between floors or landings | no limit stated for a private stair | 3835 mm (151 in) |
| Headroom over the stair | at least 6 ft 8 in (2032 mm), from the line of the nosings | at least 2 m (6 ft 6.7 in) | 2032 mm (80 in) |
| Width | at least 36 in (914 mm) clear above the handrail; 31 1/2 in (787 mm) at and below it with one handrail, 27 in with two | no general minimum for a private stair (900 mm only in a sloping-plot entrance storey); AD M's accessible category asks 850 mm (33.5 in) | 914 mm (36 in) |
| Landings | at the top and bottom; at least 36 in (914 mm) deep on a straight run | top and bottom, at least as wide and long as the flight | the larger of the two |
| Handrail | on at least one side of a flight of four or more risers, 34 to 38 in (864 to 965 mm) above the nosing line | 900 to 1000 mm (35.4 to 39.4 in) above the pitch line; on both sides if the stair is 1000 mm or wider | 900 to 965 mm (35.4 to 38 in); both sides at 1.0 m (39.4 in) and wider |
| Where a guard is required | an open side more than 30 in (762 mm) above the floor below, within 36 in (914 mm) of the edge | a drop of more than 600 mm (23.6 in) | more than 600 mm |

| Largest opening in a guard | a 4 in (102 mm) sphere must not pass; 4 3/8 in (111 mm) on a stair; 6 in (153 mm) in the triangle under a stair rail | a 100 mm (3.9 in) sphere must not pass, and no climbable horizontal rails, where children under five may live | 100 mm, with no horizontal rails |
| Lowest ceiling | habitable rooms and hallways at least 7 ft (2134 mm); bathrooms, toilets and laundries 6 ft 8 in (2032 mm) | no Building Regulations minimum found; the space standard, a planning standard and not a building regulation, asks 2.3 m (7 ft 6.6 in) over 75% of the floor | 2134 mm (84 in) |

Applied to the game (the arithmetic is under "What it means for this project"):

- **The existing staircase type breaks the IRC's riser limit and every rail rule
  of both codes.** Its 214 mm (8.44 in) riser is 18 mm over the IRC's 196 mm (it
  is inside AD K's 220 mm); its 257 mm (10.12 in) tread passes the IRC's 254 mm
  but needs a nosing the drawn steps do not have; it has no handrail (both codes
  require one, and AD K two at its 1.4 m width); and standing free with both sides
  open, it needs guards from its third tread up (AD K) or its fourth (IRC). Its
  39.8 degree pitch and 686 mm 2R+G pass AD K. Placed in today's 3 m home it also
  runs into the ceiling: headroom falls under 2 m from its fifth tread.
- **A storey of 3.0 m needs 16 risers to meet both codes:** 187.5 mm (7.38 in)
  risers on 281 mm (11.07 in) treads, 33.7 degrees, 2R+G 656 mm.
- **A 0.25 m slab under a 3.0 m storey leaves a 2.75 m (9 ft 0 in) ceiling below,
  which clears every ceiling rule found** (616 mm over the IRC's 7 ft). The plan's
  "England's 2.3 m" is correct as a number but is a planning standard, not a
  building regulation, and applies only where a local plan adopts it.

## Findings

### The US International Residential Code

**1. Which edition, and how it was read.** The ICC's own free online reader
(codes.iccsafe.org) answered every scripted request on the research day with a
captcha page, for both the 2021 and the 2024 editions, so the model code's own
pages were not read (see "Sources that could not be reached"). The 2021 text was
read in two copies that reproduce it:

- Washington State, which adopts the 2021 IRC by reference and republishes only
  the sections it amends. WAC 51-51-003, https://app.leg.wa.gov/WAC/default.aspx?cite=51-51-003,
  history "WSR 23-23-104, § 51-51-003, filed 11/15/23, effective 3/16/24":

  > "The 2021 edition of the International Residential Code as published by the
  > International Code Council is hereby adopted by reference with the following
  > additions, deletions, and exceptions"

- The Stairway Manufacturers' Association, *Visual Interpretation of the Stair
  Building Code, 2021 International Residential Code*, "© 2023 SMA Visual
  Interpretation of IRC 2021, First Edition", read as a PDF hosted by the stair
  maker Viewrail at
  https://www.viewrail.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/2021-International-Residential-Code-Visual-Interpretation.pdf.
  It reproduces the code's stair and guard sections with the ICC's permission:

  > "Portions of this publication reproduce excerpts from the 2021 International
  > Residential Code for One- and Two-Family Dwellings and the 2021 International
  > Building Code, Copyright 2021, International Code Council, Inc., Washington,
  > D.C. Reproduced with permission."

  The guide interleaves figure references ("PHOTO 13") into the code text; they
  are left out of the quotations below. Its fractions are single glyphs that did
  not survive text extraction; each is written here from the metric figure printed
  beside it (196 mm is 7 3/4 in, 787 mm is 31 1/2 in, 111 mm is 4 3/8 in).

The code is copyrighted, so only the phrases that carry each rule are quoted.

**2. Stairways, 2021 IRC section R311.7** (SMA guide, pages 5 to 18).

- R311.7.1 Width: "Stairways shall be not less than 36 inches (914 mm) in clear
  width at all points above the permitted handrail height and below the required
  headroom height." Below the handrail: "not less than 31 1/2 inches (787 mm)
  where a handrail is installed on one side and 27 inches (698 mm) where
  handrails are installed on both sides." (27 in is 686 mm; the 698 is as
  printed.)
- R311.7.2 Headroom: "The headroom in stairways shall be not less than 6 feet 8
  inches (2032 mm) measured vertically from the sloped line adjoining the tread
  nosing or from the floor surface of the landing or platform on that portion of
  the stairway."
- R311.7.3 Vertical rise: "A flight of stairs shall not have a vertical rise
  greater than 12 feet 7 inches (3835 mm) between floor levels or landings." (The
  2012 edition, quoted in a City of Bonney Lake handout, said "12 feet (3658 mm)";
  this one moved.)
- R311.7.5.1 Risers: "The riser height shall be not more than 7 3/4 inches (196
  mm)." "The greatest riser height within any flight of stairs shall not exceed
  the smallest by more than 3/8 inch (9.5 mm)." Open risers above 30 in (762 mm)
  "shall not permit the passage of a 4-inch-diameter (102 mm) sphere".
- R311.7.5.2 Treads: "The tread depth shall be not less than 10 inches (254 mm)."
- R311.7.5.3 Nosings: "A nosing projection not less than 3/4 inch (19 mm) and not
  more than 1 1/4 inches (32 mm) shall be provided on stairways." "Exception: A
  nosing projection is not required where the tread depth is not less than 11
  inches (279 mm)."
- R311.7.6 Landings: "There shall be a floor or landing at the top and bottom of
  each stairway." "Where the stairway has a straight run, the depth in the
  direction of travel shall be not less than 36 inches (914 mm)." Its exception: a
  landing "is not required at the top of an interior flight of stairs ... provided
  a door does not swing over the stairs."
- R311.7.8 Handrails: "Handrails shall be provided on not less than one side of
  each flight of stairs with four or more risers."
- R311.7.8.1 Height: "Handrail height, measured vertically from the sloped plane
  adjoining the tread nosing, or finish surface of ramp slope, shall be not less
  than 34 inches (864 mm) and not more than 38 inches (965 mm)."
- R311.7.8.2: handrails "shall not project more than 4 1/2 inches (114 mm) on
  either side of the stairway" (Boise's handrail handout #436, dated "12/2022",
  prints the same).

The IRC states no pitch limit of its own. The steepest stair its riser and tread
allow is 196 mm over 254 mm, 37.7 degrees.

**3. Guards, 2021 IRC section R312.1** (SMA guide, pages 23 and 24).

- R312.1.1 Where required: "Guards shall be provided for those portions of
  open-sided walking surfaces, including floors, stairs, ramps and landings, that
  are located more than 30 inches (762 mm) measured vertically to the floor or
  grade below at any point within 36 inches (914 mm) horizontally to the edge of
  the open side."
- R312.1.2 Height: "not less than 36 inches (914 mm) in height as measured
  vertically above the adjacent walking surface or the line connecting the
  nosings." Exception 1: "Guards on the open sides of stairs shall have a height
  of not less than 34 inches (864 mm) measured vertically from a line connecting
  the nosings." Exception 2: where the guard's top serves as the stair's handrail,
  between 34 and 38 inches (864 and 965 mm).
- R312.1.3 Opening limitations: guards "shall not have openings from the walking
  surface to the required guard height that allow passage of a sphere 4 inches
  (102 mm) in diameter." Exceptions: the triangle formed by riser, tread and a
  stair guard's bottom rail, "a sphere 6 inches (153 mm) in diameter"; guards on
  the open side of stairs, "a sphere 4 3/8 inches (111 mm) in diameter".

Washington's own R312.1.1 (WAC 51-51-0312, https://app.leg.wa.gov/WAC/default.aspx?cite=51-51-0312,
"WSR 23-23-104, § 51-51-0312, filed 11/15/23, effective 3/16/24") reads the same
with lofts added, which shows a state amending the model text:

> "Guards shall be provided for those portions of open-sided walking surfaces,
> including floors, mezzanines, lofts in accordance with Section R333, stairs,
> ramps, and landings, that are located more than 30 inches (762 mm) measured
> vertically to the floor or grade below"

and its R312.1.2 keeps "not less than 36 inches (914 mm) in height". (A search
also turned up a San Luis Obispo handout said to give 42 inches from a 2019
California code; it was not read, so it is not used here.)

**4. Ceiling height, IRC section R305.1.** Not reproduced by the SMA guide, and
not amended by Washington (WAC 51-51-0305 does not exist; the request returned
the chapter index). Read in the City of Boise's code handout #434, dated
"01/2022", https://www.cityofboise.org/media/3924/434-irc-code-section-r305-ceiling-height.pdf,
which quotes the section without naming its edition:

> "Habitable space, hallways and portions of basements containing these spaces
> shall have a ceiling height of not less than 7 feet (2134 mm). Bathrooms,
> toilet rooms and laundry rooms shall have a ceiling height of not less than 6
> feet 8 inches (2032 mm)."

Its exceptions include a sloped-ceiling rule (at least half the required floor
area at 7 ft, none under 5 ft) and, over a shower, 6 ft 8 in above a 30 in by 30 in
area at the showerhead.

Minnesota amends it upward. Minnesota Rules 1309.0305, https://www.revisor.mn.gov/rules/1309.0305/,
"Published Electronically: January 26, 2015", under rule 1309.0010's adoption of
"The 2018 edition of the International Residential Code":

> "R305.1 Minimum height, new buildings. Habitable space, hallways, bathrooms,
> toilet rooms, laundry rooms, and portions of basements containing these spaces
> shall have a ceiling height of not less than 7 feet (2134 mm)."

**5. The 2024 edition renumbered the stair sections.** The Minnesota Department
of Labor and Industry's notes of its Residential Code advisory group, meeting 6,
"Date: 1/22/2025", https://dli.mn.gov/sites/default/files/pdf/TAG-residential-012225-notes.pdf,
review "2024 IRC sections R318.3.2 through R319.5", including:

> "Section R318.7.2 Stairway headroom. The TAG consensus is to accept the model
> code language as written"

So in the 2024 IRC the stairway rules sit under R318.7, not R311.7. Where the
2024 edition puts handrails and guards was not confirmed: a forum summary says a
code change created a separate handrail section, but that is not the code. The
2024 wording and numbers were not read (see below).

### England

**6. Approved Document K, 2013 edition.** Ministry of Housing, Communities and
Local Government, *Protection from falling, collision and impact: Approved
Document K*, gov.uk page https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/protection-from-falling-collision-and-impact-approved-document-k,
"Published: 3 January 2013"; the PDF is
https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/60d5bdcde90e07716f516cfd/Approved_Document_K.pdf,
"2013 edition", which "takes effect on 6 April 2013 for use in England". Crown
copyright; quoted for what each rule says.

- Table 1.1, rise and going, private stair: rise minimum 150 mm, maximum 220 mm;
  going minimum 220 mm, maximum 300 mm (the table's columns were read from the
  PDF's plain-text extraction, which keeps them in order). Its notes: "The maximum
  pitch for a private stair is 42°." (the degree sign is a glyph the extraction
  lost; nothing else fits) and "The normal relationship between the dimensions of
  the rise and going is: twice the rise plus the going (2R + G) equals between
  550mm and 700mm."
- 1.9, open risers in dwellings: "a. Overlap treads by a minimum of 16mm. b.
  Construct the steps so that a 100mm diameter sphere cannot pass through the open
  risers."
- 1.11 and Diagram 1.3, headroom, for all buildings: "On the access between
  levels, provide the minimum headroom shown in Diagram 1.3"; the diagram marks
  "At least 2m" over the flight's pitch line and over each landing. (1.13 allows
  1.9 m, falling to 1.8 m at the side, in a loft conversion only.)
- Width: the minimum widths in 1.15 are "For buildings other than dwellings". For
  dwellings the only width given is 1.16, where a sloping plot forces steps inside
  the entrance storey: "a minimum stair width of 900mm". So AD K sets no general
  minimum width for a private stair. A negative finding, recorded so it is not
  re-researched.
- 1.20, landings: "At the top and bottom of every flight, provide landings the
  width and length at least as great as the smallest width of the flight".
- 1.34, handrails, for all buildings: "a. Position the top of the handrail 900mm
  to 1000mm from the pitch line or floor. b. The handrail may form the top of a
  guarding if you can match the heights. c. If the stairs are 1000mm or wider:
  provide a handrail on both sides."
- 1.41, guarding of stairs, "In dwellings": "Provide guarding at the sides of
  flights and landings when there is a drop of more than 600mm." And K2's
  performance statement: "in dwellings: provide pedestrian guarding that is
  capable of preventing people from being injured by falling from a height of
  more than 600mm".
- 3.1, where guarding goes: "the edges of any part of a floor (including the edge
  below an opening window), gallery, balcony, roof (including roof lights and
  other openings), any other place to which people have access". Its note: no
  guarding is needed "in places such as loading bays where it would obstruct
  normal use."
- 3.2 and Diagram 3.1, guard height: for "Single family dwellings", "Stairs,
  landings, ramps, edges of internal floors" 900 mm, and "External balconies,
  including Juliette balconies and edges of roof" 1100 mm. And 3.2(b): "You can
  use any wall, parapet, balustrade or similar obstruction as guarding."
- 1.39 and 3.3, openings, where children under five may live: "ensure that a
  100mm sphere cannot pass through any openings in the guarding" and "To prevent
  children from readily being able to climb the guarding: avoid horizontal rails."
- Ramps, 2.3 and Diagram 2.1, for all buildings: the steepest ramp allowed falls
  with its going, "1:12 (166mm rise)" at 2 m, "1:15 (333mm rise)" at 5 m, "1:20
  (500mm rise)" at 10 m, and "it is acceptable to interpolate between the maximum
  gradients i.e. 1:14 for 4m going or 1:19 for 9m going". And 2.12, "In
  dwellings": "For ramps that are 600mm or less in height: you do not need to
  provide handrails."

**7. Approved Document M, volume 1: dwellings, 2015 edition incorporating 2016
amendments.** gov.uk page https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/access-to-and-use-of-buildings-approved-document-m,
"Published: 1 March 2015", "Last updated: 1 October 2024"; the volume 1 PDF is
https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a7f8a82ed915d74e622b17b/BR_PDF_AD_M1_2015_with_2016_amendments_V3.pdf.
It bears on this question twice:

- Stair width, in the accessible and adaptable category (M4(2)), 2.23(c): "The
  stair from the entrance storey to the storey above (or below) has a minimum
  clear width of 850mm when measured 450mm above the pitch line of the treads
  (ignoring any newel post)." and (d) "All stairs meet the provisions of Part K
  for private stairs."
- A WC on the entrance storey. The default category (M4(1), visitable), 1.17(a):
  "A room (which may be a WC/cloakroom or a bathroom) containing a WC is provided
  on the entrance storey". M4(2), 2.27(a): "Every dwelling has a room within the
  entrance storey that provides a WC and basin".

M4(2) applies only where planning asks for it, 0.3: "Requirements M4(2) and M4(3)
are 'optional requirements' as defined in the Building Regulations. An optional
requirement only applies where a condition that one or more dwellings should
meet the relevant optional requirement is imposed on new development as part of
the process of granting planning permission."

Approved Document F (ventilation) was not read: it sets no stair or ceiling-height
rule that this question needs.

**8. England's 2.3 m ceiling is a planning standard, not a building regulation.**
Department for Communities and Local Government, *Technical housing standards:
nationally described space standard*, "March 2015" (notes "added 19 May 2016"),
https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6123c60e8fa8f53dd1f9b04d/160519_Nationally_Described_Space_Standard.pdf,
gov.uk page "Published: 27 March 2015":

> "i. the minimum floor to ceiling height is 2.3m for at least 75% of the Gross
> Internal Area"

and the gov.uk page:

> "It is not a building regulation and remains solely within the planning system
> as a new form of technical planning standard."

The standard's own paragraph 6 says it is used "when designing new homes and
seeking planning approval (if a local authority has adopted the space standard in
its Local Plan)". Neither AD K nor AD M volume 1 gives a minimum ceiling height
for a room: a search of both for "ceiling" finds only AD M's hoist-strength rule
for bedroom and bathroom ceilings in wheelchair dwellings, and AD K's cleaning
access to glazed ceilings.

### OSHA, for work platforms only

**9. US workplace rules (29 CFR 1910 subpart D).** Read in the eCFR, title 29
"up to date as of 2026-10-19", through its renderer at
https://www.ecfr.gov/api/renderer/v1/content/enhanced/current/title-29?part=1910&section=1910.25
(and the same for 1910.28 and 1910.29); histories "[81 FR 82981, Nov. 18, 2016, as
amended at 84 FR 68795, Dec. 17, 2019]" (1910.25) and "... 84 FR 68796, Dec. 17,
2019]" (1910.29). These bind employers, not homes; they matter here only for a
work platform such as a future greenhouse walkway or vehicle bay gantry.

- 1910.25(b)(2): headroom "at least 6 feet, 8 inches (203 cm), as measured from
  the leading edge of the tread"; (c) standard stairs: "installed at angles between
  30 to 50 degrees from the horizontal", "a maximum riser height of 9.5 inches (24
  cm)", "a minimum tread depth of 9.5 inches (24 cm)", "a minimum width of 22
  inches (56 cm)".
- 1910.28(b)(1)(i): fall protection for "an unprotected side or edge that is 4 feet
  (1.2 m) or more above a lower level".
- 1910.29(b)(1): guardrail top rails "42 inches (107 cm), plus or minus 3 inches (8
  cm), above the walking-working surface"; (b)(2)(iii) balusters "no more than 19
  inches (48 cm) apart".

## What it means for this project

### Certain (arithmetic on the sources above)

**The existing staircase type** (`data/blueprints/structure_types.ron`, "stairs":
size (1.4, 3.0, 3.6), 14 steps; the Steps shape in `src/ship/structure.rs` stacks
14 full-height boxes, the last flush with the top, with no nosing and no rails):

| Measure | The game's stairs | IRC 2021 | AD K 2013 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Riser | 3.0 / 14 = 214.3 mm (8.44 in) | breaks: over 196 mm by 18.3 mm | meets: under 220 mm by 5.7 mm |
| Tread / going | 3.6 / 14 = 257.1 mm (10.12 in) | meets 254 mm (by 3.1 mm); breaks R311.7.5.3, which asks a 19 to 32 mm nosing below 279 mm, and the drawn steps have none | meets 220 to 300 mm |
| Pitch | atan(214.3 / 257.1) = 39.8 degrees | no limit of its own | meets 42 degrees |
| 2R + G | 428.6 + 257.1 = 685.7 mm (27.0 in) | no rule | meets 550 to 700 mm |
| Rise of the flight | 3.0 m (9 ft 10 in) | meets 3835 mm | no limit |
| Width | 1.4 m (55.1 in) | meets 914 mm | no minimum; meets AD M's 850 mm |
| Handrails | none | breaks: required on one side (14 risers is four or more) | breaks: required, and on both sides at 1.4 m wide |
| Guards (stairs-1 stands free, both sides open) | none | breaks from the 4th tread (4 x 214 = 857 mm, over 762) | breaks from the 3rd tread (643 mm, over 600) |
| Landing at the top | none (it leads to nothing) | breaks R311.7.6 | breaks 1.20 |
| Headroom as placed (today's 3.0 m roof) | 3.0 m less the nosing line: 1.93 m (6 ft 4 in) at the 5th tread, zero at the top | breaks from the 5th tread (2032 mm) | breaks from the 5th tread (2.0 m) |

**What a 3.0 m storey needs.** The IRC's 196 mm riser asks at least 3000 / 196 =
15.3, so 16 risers: 187.5 mm (7.38 in). A tread of at least 279 mm lets the steps
be drawn without a nosing (R311.7.5.3's exception), and AD K caps it at 300 mm;
281.25 mm (11.07 in) gives a run of 16 x 281.25 = 4.5 m (14 ft 9 in, the last
tread flush with the upper floor as the Steps shape draws it), a pitch of
atan(187.5 / 281.25) = 33.7 degrees, and 2R+G = 656 mm (25.8 in), inside every
rule above.

**The plan's 3.0 m floor to floor with a 0.25 m slab** leaves 2.75 m (9 ft 0 in)
below: 616 mm (24 in) over the IRC's 7 ft for habitable rooms, 718 mm over its
6 ft 8 in for bathrooms, and 450 mm over the space standard's 2.3 m. With the shell
roof at 6.0 m, the upper storey's ceiling is 3.0 m (9 ft 10 in). A stair under
such a slab keeps 2032 mm of headroom only while the nosing line is no higher than
2.75 - 2.032 = 0.718 m, so the slab must be open above every part of the flight
beyond that height. With the stair above, that is 0.796 m (31 in) along the flight
from its foot (0.844 m under AD K's 2.0 m).

### Judgement calls (ours, not the sources')

- **The game takes the stricter of the two codes wherever they differ**, because a
  home that passes both is a fair teaching model and the stricter numbers cost the
  design nothing it needs: a 196 mm riser cap, a 280 mm-class tread with no nosing,
  2032 mm headroom, a guard wherever a walkable edge drops more than 600 mm, guards
  at least 1.0 m (39.4 in) tall on floor edges (over both 900 and 914 mm), handrails
  at 0.93 m (36.6 in) above the nosing line (inside both 864 to 965 and 900 to 1000)
  on both sides of a stair 1.0 m or wider, and guard openings under 100 mm or a
  solid panel.
- **A wall counts as a guard** (AD K 3.2(b)), so where the game has no rail piece
  yet, a wall or a solid glass panel of guard height is the data-only way to meet
  the guard rules. Nothing in the game draws a handrail or a sloped stair guard yet:
  that is a request for code, not something data can do.
- **The game's existing ramp (1.2 m over 4.5 m, 1:3.75) is about four times as
  steep as AD K allows at that going (1:14.5)**, so its note's "wheelchair friendly"
  is not true. That is arithmetic; what to do about it is in
  [the upper floor layout](../../design/homestead-upper-floor.md).
- **OSHA is a guide for the work platforms the plan defers** (the greenhouse
  walkway, a vehicle bay gantry): it would ask a 42 in (1.07 m) rail where an edge
  is 4 ft (1.2 m) or more above the floor below. A home is not a workplace, so it is
  not applied to the house.
